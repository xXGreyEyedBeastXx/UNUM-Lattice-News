# Dissent, Reform, and Internal Correction Diagnostic v0.1

Status: active review
Analyst seat: Vella Cora — News Analyst

## Purpose

Evaluate what a group, institution, movement, ideology, religion, party, corporation, state, or community actually protects by examining how it treats internal dissent, reform, whistleblowing, defections, moral criticism, and corrective pressure.

This diagnostic is cross-lattice. It is not specific to Zionism, Christianity, Islam, leftist movements, corporations, governments, or activist organizations.

## Core proposition

```text
An ideology tells us what it claims to value.
Its treatment of internal reformers tells us what it protects under pressure.
```

Internal dissent is not automatically correct. A dissenter may be mistaken, malicious, compromised, abusive, or acting in bad faith. The diagnostic therefore evaluates both the content of dissent and the system response.

## Distinct dissent roles

Do not collapse all internal critics into one category.

Possible roles include:

- reformer;
- whistleblower;
- conscientious objector;
- internal moral critic;
- policy dissenter;
- doctrinal dissenter;
- minority faction;
- defecting member;
- expelled member;
- harmed participant;
- professional/scientific critic;
- anti-corruption investigator;
- opposition current;
- internal opposition media;
- externalized former insider.

## Core diagnostic questions

For each case ask:

1. What exactly did the dissenter challenge?
2. Was the challenge factual, moral, procedural, ideological, strategic, or personal?
3. What evidence did the dissenter provide?
4. Did the organization investigate the substance?
5. Was criticism permitted without identity expulsion?
6. Was the dissenter answered, ignored, mocked, censored, demoted, fired, expelled, prosecuted, threatened, surveilled, or physically harmed?
7. Were accusations such as betrayal, disloyalty, heresy, treason, extremism, sabotage, antisemitism, Islamophobia, racism, sexism, counterrevolution, anti-nationalism, or similar labels used? If so, were they evidence-based or defensive framing?
8. Did the response distinguish harmful conduct from disagreement?
9. Was there an appeal or review mechanism?
10. Could the organization admit error without collapsing its identity?
11. Did policy actually change after valid criticism?
12. Were harmed people repaired or compensated?
13. Did dissenters remain members after correction, or was belonging conditioned on conformity?
14. Did leadership face the same scrutiny as rank-and-file members?
15. Did the system preserve a route for re-entry?

## Response spectrum

Possible response states:

```text
welcomes criticism
  -> protects dissent
  -> investigates substance
  -> tolerates disagreement
  -> contains disagreement without resolution
  -> stigmatizes dissent
  -> isolates or marginalizes dissenters
  -> punishes dissent institutionally
  -> criminalizes dissent
  -> treats dissent as enemy activity
  -> coerces, threatens, imprisons, or uses violence
```

This is not a simple score. Record mechanisms and evidence.

## Correction permeability

A useful derived concept is **correction permeability**: how easily accurate criticism can cross the group's defensive boundary and produce durable change.

High correction permeability may include:

- protected channels for criticism;
- independent review;
- transparent corrections;
- leadership accountability;
- preservation of minority positions;
- reversible decisions;
- public acknowledgement of error;
- restoration after unjust punishment.

Low correction permeability may include:

- loyalty tests;
- leader infallibility;
- ideological purity enforcement;
- retaliation against whistleblowers;
- suppression of counterevidence;
- forced unanimity;
- reputational destruction of internal critics;
- redefining criticism as identity betrayal;
- punishment without independent appeal;
- historical rewriting after failure.

## Capture signal

Repeated punishment of dissent can indicate capture, but the inference must be typed.

```text
internal criticism
  -> defensive identity response
  -> dissent classified as betrayal
  -> information bottleneck
  -> leadership insulation
  -> error persistence
  -> institutional capture / ideological rigidity risk
```

Do not infer capture from one conflict alone. Look for recurrence, asymmetry, leadership insulation, suppression of evidence, and failure to correct known harms.

## Reform stress test

For ideologies and movements, ask:

> What happens when adherents try to remove domination, cruelty, corruption, coercion, hierarchy, or factual error from the project?

Possible outcomes:

1. **Integration:** reform is incorporated and the movement changes.
2. **Plural coexistence:** reform remains a legitimate internal current.
3. **Marginal tolerance:** reform exists but has little institutional influence.
4. **Boundary expulsion:** reformers are treated as no longer authentic members.
5. **Suppression:** reform is actively punished or eliminated.
6. **Semantic departure:** reformers retain the label while abandoning the ideology's operative core.

This is particularly useful when evaluating whether an ideology's harmful feature is incidental or structurally central.

## Cross-scene applications

Potential uses include:

- Zionist treatment of binational or anti-domination currents;
- Christian-nationalist treatment of Christian internal critics;
- Islamist or theocratic treatment of Muslim reformers;
- leftist movements' treatment of revisionists, anarchists, whistleblowers, and internal opposition;
- political parties' treatment of dissenting legislators;
- corporations' treatment of whistleblowers and safety critics;
- governments' treatment of journalists and inspectors;
- humanitarian organizations' treatment of internal abuse/corruption reports;
- scientific institutions' treatment of methodological dissent;
- UNUM/News treatment of criticism of its own models.

## Core locks

```text
Dissent is not automatically truth.
Conformity is not automatically loyalty.
Criticism is not automatically betrayal.
A group's treatment of critics is evidence about its power structure.
Valid reform must be allowed to change the model.
No organization may be sole judge of its own harms.
Internal critics retain dignity even when they are wrong.
Whistleblower protection does not erase accountability for false claims.
Re-entry matters.
Apply this diagnostic to us too.
```
