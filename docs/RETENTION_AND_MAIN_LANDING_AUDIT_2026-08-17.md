## What `merged` means here

This audit deliberately distinguishes three cases:

1. **Directly/semantically landed content** — surviving content was accepted into current canonical state.
2. **Already landed** — branch history contributed no unique commits beyond main.
3. **Superseded but preserved** — branch history was joined into main ancestry while canonical content intentionally rejected or replaced the branch implementation.

Therefore:

```text
BRANCH_IS_ANCESTOR_OF_MAIN
!= EVERY_FILE_VERSION_FROM_BRANCH_IS_CANONICAL
```

That distinction prevents Git history from becoming an accidental ontology.
