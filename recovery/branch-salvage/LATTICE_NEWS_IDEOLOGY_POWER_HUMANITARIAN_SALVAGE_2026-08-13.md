
```text
difference
```

## 4. Humanitarian Scene — recovered analytic spine

**Source branch:** `organic-lattice-sorting-2026-08-11`  
**Original path:** `research/scenes/HUMANITARIAN_SCENE_v0_1.md`  
**Original blob SHA:** `1b0aae10e517350da9d85a3cb86ad275675d6304`

The humanitarian scene maps actors, institutions, resources, constraints, and harms involved in preserving life, dignity, relief access, civilian agency, and recovery across conflict, disaster, occupation, repression, poverty, displacement, and ecological crisis.

It is explicitly **not a moral caste**: humanitarian actors can protect, fail, become captured, reproduce harmful power, or be deliberately obstructed.

Flow map:

```text
need
-> recognition / assessment
-> funding / authorization
-> procurement
-> transport / access
-> local distribution
-> actual receipt
-> outcome
-> monitoring / correction / re-entry
```

At each crossing, ask who controls passage; what is delayed, denied, diverted, taxed, confiscated, conditioned, or destroyed; what documentation is required; which populations become invisible; and what evidence of actual delivery survives.

The scene separates claimed neutrality from actual power relations and distinguishes humanitarian effectiveness from trust or morality.

Hard distinctions:

```text
humanitarian actor != neutral by definition
victim population != passive population
state actor != lawful by default
armed group != terrorist by default
terrorist designation != proof of every alleged act
aid obstruction != automatically military necessity
security rationale != automatically pretext
foreign funding != automatic capture
religious identity != humanitarian reliability
```

Tiny lock:

> Protect the harmed signal without turning suffering into propaganda. Follow aid all the way from promise to receipt. Follow obstruction all the way from justification to consequence. Preserve local agency. No flag, faith, institution, donor, army, militia, or charity receives immunity from evidence.
