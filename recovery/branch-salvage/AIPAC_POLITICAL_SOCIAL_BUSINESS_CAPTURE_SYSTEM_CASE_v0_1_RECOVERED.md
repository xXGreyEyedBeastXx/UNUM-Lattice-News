## Core question

How does AIPAC and the broader pro-Israel political influence network convert money, access, endorsements, independent expenditures, lobbying, donor relationships, institutional alliances, and public narratives into durable political power in the United States, and what harms, benefits, constraints, counterforces, and democratic effects follow?

## Evidence classes

Separate direct campaign contributions, independent expenditures, lobbying disclosures, endorsements, donor networks, legislative sponsorship/voting, executive access, campus effects, anti-boycott legislation, antisemitism-definition policy, contractor/funding conditions, advertising strategy, business relationships, philanthropy, public statements, pressure/retaliation claims, documented outcomes, counterevidence, and failed influence attempts.

## Durable method

For electoral cases record race, candidate, official endorsement, PAC contributions, independent expenditures, other donor-linked groups, advertising themes, whether Israel appears in ads, candidate Israel/Gaza position, outcome, and counterfactual evidence. Do not infer coordination between candidates and legally independent super PACs without evidence.

For legislation distinguish sponsor/cosponsors, documented AIPAC position, other supporting organizations, constitutional/civil-liberties concerns, antisemitism-protection rationale, Israel-policy effect, status, votes, and outcomes. Legislative alignment is not automatically evidence of AIPAC causation.

For antisemitism/speech analysis preserve both documented antisemitic harassment/violence and documented or credibly alleged conflation of criticism of Israel/Zionism with antisemitism or suppression of protected speech.

For donor/business mapping, do not use Jewish identity as a proxy for network membership. Map only documented donor, organizational, business, board, philanthropic, contract, and Israel-business relations.

## Social/institutional capture hypothesis

Working hypothesis, not conclusion:

```text
large donor base
 -> campaign finance capacity
 -> candidate selection / electoral pressure
 -> legislative access
 -> policy reinforcement
 -> institutional signaling
 -> donor/philanthropic pressure
 -> speech/norm enforcement
 -> lower political cost for pro-Israel policy
 -> higher political cost for dissent
```

Every arrow must be tested independently.

Alternative models include ordinary interest-group influence; high-intensity single-issue coordination without capture; broader U.S. strategic/ideological support that would persist without AIPAC; Christian Zionist and defense/geopolitical networks as coequal/stronger drivers; and partisan/donor preferences that AIPAC amplifies rather than causes.
