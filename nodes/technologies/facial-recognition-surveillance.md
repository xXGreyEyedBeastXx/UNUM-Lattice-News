# Facial Recognition Surveillance

**Node type:** technology / surveillance mechanism  
**Status:** deployed  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-09

## Current bounded summary

Commercial facial-recognition services are used by U.S. federal law-enforcement agencies to search very large image collections and assist in identifying people. A 2023 U.S. Government Accountability Office review found that seven selected agencies in the Departments of Homeland Security and Justice reported using such services, which can search billions of facial images.

The GAO also found gaps in training and policies intended to protect civil rights and civil liberties.

This node establishes current institutional use and oversight concerns. It does not establish that every facial-recognition match is acted upon, that every agency uses the same system, or that every use is unlawful.

## Direct relations

- Selected DHS and DOJ agencies **USE** commercial or nonprofit facial-recognition services.
- GAO **REVIEWS / WARNS_ABOUT** training and policy gaps affecting civil-rights and civil-liberties protections.
- Facial recognition **AFFECTS** people whose images are included, searched, or matched, although the scope and consequences vary by system and use case.

## Evidence posture

### Observed / directly documented

- Seven selected federal law-enforcement agencies reported using external facial-recognition services.
- The services can search billions of images.
- At the time covered by the GAO review, training and agency-specific policy requirements were incomplete.

### Supported concern

A system that can rapidly search vast image collections changes the practical balance between public anonymity, investigative power, and meaningful review. The exact consequence depends on match quality, human verification, legal authority, retention, downstream use, and appeal mechanisms.

## Unknowns and disputes

- Current agency-by-agency training and policy status after the 2023 review.
- Error rates and demographic performance of each service in actual agency use.
- How often a match contributes to detention, search, charging, or other consequential action.
- What remedies are available when a match is wrong or an image was obtained without meaningful consent.
