# Reproductive Autonomy — Living Dossier

## Purpose

This dossier is the durable local view for reproductive-autonomy relations. It deepens, but does not replace, the broad `docs/CONTROVERSY_MEMBRANE_v0_1.md` registry.

The domain should remain navigable from multiple directions:

```text
reproductive autonomy
<-> pregnant person / minor / family
<-> law / policy
<-> court / enforcement institution
<-> medical system / provider
<-> surveillance / data infrastructure
<-> ideology / movement / religious network
<-> donor / lobbying / political actor
<-> documented protection / harm / material outcome
```

No single dossier owns the relation. Canonical stitches and bridges may be visible from several node-local views.

## Declared ethical lens: bodily sovereignty

The project uses a bodily-sovereignty lens in this domain:

```text
no person has an automatic right to use, occupy, alter, or control another person's body without consent
```

Pregnancy can create competing moral interests, especially as fetal development advances, but those interests do not automatically convert into ownership or compulsory use of the pregnant person's body.

Where fetal survival outside the pregnant person's body becomes medically feasible, and where delivery can be accomplished without imposing disproportionate risk, it may be ethically or clinically **recommended** to consider delivery, induction, or cesarean delivery as alternatives to ending fetal life.

`RECOMMENDED != FORCED`

`CLINICALLY_PREFERABLE_IN_A_CASE != LEGALLY_MANDATED`

No recommendation in this dossier authorizes forced surgery, forced cesarean delivery, compelled pregnancy continuation, or other non-consensual medical intervention.

Medical feasibility, gestational viability, maternal risk, fetal condition, and safest delivery route are clinical questions and should be sourced as such rather than inferred from ideology.

If adoption is invoked as an alternative after birth, the ethical analysis must continue downstream into the actual adoption, foster-care, disability-support, maternal-care, and child-welfare systems. A recommendation to carry or deliver for adoption is incomplete if the receiving social system is unsafe, exploitative, inaccessible, or materially unsupported.

## Core distinctions

Do not collapse:

- `ABORTION_RIGHTS` — legal or medical access to abortion;
- `ABORTION_RESTRICTION` — gestational, procedural, funding, provider, or parental-involvement restrictions;
- `ABORTION_BAN` — total or near-total statutory prohibition;
- `EXTREMIST_ENFORCEMENT` — a separately evidenced status for punitive, violent, surveillance-oriented, or other extreme enforcement mechanisms; ordinary abortion opposition is not automatically extremism;
- `CONTRACEPTION_RESTRICTION` — legal, institutional, insurance, provider-refusal, or personhood-based constraints on contraception;
- `REPRODUCTIVE_SURVEILLANCE` — reporting, data, civil-enforcement, investigative, or cross-border enforcement mechanisms;
- `MINOR_FORCED_CONTINUATION` — use only when the legal/practical structure actually removes a feasible lawful abortion pathway; do not infer individual outcomes without evidence.

## Relation endpoint rule

Every mature relation should terminate at the participant or ecology actually affected:

```text
claimed protection / restriction
-> actual statutory, medical, financial, or enforcement mechanism
-> authority or private enforcement seat
-> practical access pathway
-> pregnant person / minor / helper / provider / family
-> documented protection, burden, harm, or unresolved outcome
```

## Minors and privacy

Material involving minors receives heightened privacy protection. Do not surface identifying information merely because a source published it. Track law, policy, court procedure, access constraints, and documented outcomes without turning a minor into an investigative target.

## Surveillance lane

Treat reproductive surveillance as a mechanism family, not a slogan. Distinguish:

- lawful request or subpoena;
- commercial data-broker exposure;
- platform disclosure;
- private civil enforcement;
- criminal investigation;
- cross-state or travel-related enforcement;
- proposed capability versus documented use.

Legal authorization does not erase the need to record downstream harm, and capability does not establish intent or actual deployment.

## Contraception lane

Separate:

- outright prohibition, if documented;
- conscience or provider-refusal law;
- insurance or funding restriction;
- emergency-contraception access;
- personhood/fertilization definitions and their actual legal effect;
- rhetoric describing contraception as abortifacient;
- affirmative contraception protections.

Contested medical claims must remain typed as contested unless evidence establishes them.

## Evidence locks

`Anti-abortion != extremist.`

`Civil statutory damages != government bounty payment, even when they create private enforcement incentives.`

`No explicit contraception ban != no contraception pressure.`

`Personhood rhetoric != proven contraception prohibition unless statutory or adjudicated mechanism supports the relation.`

`Protection claim != demonstrated protective outcome.`

`Recommendation != coercive authority.`

`Viability != permission for forced surgery.`

`Adoption option != humane endpoint unless the downstream care system is evaluated.`

`Legal != harmless.`

`Harm allegation != documented harm without evidence.`
