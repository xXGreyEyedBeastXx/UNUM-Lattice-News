# Corrections and Living Record

UNUM Lattice News treats correction as part of evidence integrity rather than as an embarrassment to hide.

When a published claim is materially wrong, incomplete, overstated, outdated, or contradicted by stronger evidence:

1. correct the affected material;
2. identify the substantive change when practical;
3. preserve the date of correction;
4. add or replace supporting sources;
5. state what remains unresolved;
6. avoid silently rewriting consequential conclusions.

Minor spelling, formatting, or link repairs do not require a formal correction note unless they change meaning.

A correction does not automatically invalidate an entire report, and an earlier error does not justify preserving a claim after better evidence arrives.

## Article and rendition retirement

Articles, briefings, and other reader-facing renditions are outputs of the lattice, not the durable causal substrate.

An old rendition may be revised, superseded, retired, or deleted when it is materially stale, evidentially weak, causally flattened, contaminated by moral/reputation laundering, dependent on obsolete schema, or otherwise no longer trustworthy enough to serve as a public surface.

Before retiring or deleting a consequential rendition, recover any unique durable material that still matters:

```text
source lineage
exact dates / clocks
claims and claim posture
state transitions
actor / authority seats
typed relations
contribution records
harm / consequence edges
knowledge / notice / persistence state
claim-specific evidence effects
missingness / unresolved questions
correction and provenance lineage
```

Preserve the causal web, not obsolete prose.

```text
article != evidence container of last resort
old wording != canonical truth
historical publication != methodological authority
retiring prose != erasing verified evidence
keeping provenance != keeping laundering
```

If the old rendition contains no unique evidence, relation, transition, correction, or provenance object that survives current review, it does not need to remain merely for sentiment or continuity.

If a retired rendition contained consequential errors or laundering, preserve enough correction/provenance metadata to explain why it was retired when practical. Do not silently resurrect its framing through summaries, agents, or later articles.

## Suggested correction note

```text
Correction — YYYY-MM-DD

Previous posture:
Updated posture:
Reason:
New or corrected evidence:
What remains unresolved:
```

## Challenges and claim-specific contradictory evidence

Good-faith challenges, contradictory primary evidence, source-quality concerns, and other evidence that could weaken an existing conclusion must be evaluated on their merits regardless of whether they support or weaken the current assessment.

Do not route them through a generic moral or reputational `counterevidence` bucket. For consequential work, identify the exact target and evidentiary function under the current evidence-jurisdiction grammar, such as:

```text
CLAIM_DEFEATING
SCOPE_LIMITING
MOTIVE_LIMITING
RELATION_LIMITING
ALTERNATIVE_SUPPORTING
RELIABILITY_MODIFYING
CONSTRAINT_RESISTANCE
REALIZED_OUTCOME
NON_RESPONSIVE
```

A denial is a stated position unless evidence gives it another function. Defensiveness is response behavior, not factual rebuttal by itself. A benevolent policy name is framing, not evidence that the operative mechanism was benevolent. A charitable act, beneficial program, favorable biography, institutional mission, or good outcome elsewhere is `NON_RESPONSIVE` to an unrelated harm claim.

```text
unrelated good conduct != claim-specific contradictory evidence
protective rhetoric != proof of protective mechanism
policy name != policy effect
denial != correction
defensiveness != exculpation
successful resistance != erasure of attempted harm
```

The purpose of the record is not to win every argument or soften every conclusion. It is to become harder to fool, harder to capture, harder to launder, and easier to repair.
