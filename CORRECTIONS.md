# Corrections and Living Record

UNUM Lattice News treats correction as part of evidence integrity rather than as an embarrassment to hide.

When a published claim is materially wrong, incomplete, overstated, outdated, or contradicted by stronger evidence:

1. correct the affected material;
2. identify the substantive change when practical;
3. preserve the date of correction;
4. add or replace supporting sources;
5. state what remains unresolved;
6. avoid silently rewriting consequential conclusions.

Minor spelling, formatting, or link repairs do not require a formal correction note unless they change meaning.

A correction does not automatically invalidate an entire report, and an earlier error does not justify preserving a claim after better evidence arrives.

## Suggested correction note

```text
Correction — YYYY-MM-DD

Previous posture:
Updated posture:
Reason:
New or corrected evidence:
What remains unresolved:
```

## Challenges and counterevidence

Good-faith challenges, contradictory primary evidence, and source-quality concerns should be evaluated on their merits regardless of whether they support or weaken an existing conclusion.

The purpose of the record is not to win every argument. It is to become harder to fool, harder to capture, and easier to repair.
