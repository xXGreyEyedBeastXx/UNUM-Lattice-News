## Method

A stated government rationale is recorded as `STATED`; it is not automatically evidence of necessity, legitimacy, proportionality, or public benefit.

## 1. Immigration detention -> private profit -> captive labor

### Observed
- ICE used emergency/urgency authority and no-bid or modified contracts to rapidly expand detention capacity under the second Trump administration.
- CoreCivic and GEO Group projected hundreds of millions in additional revenue from the expansion; both companies' share prices rose sharply after Trump's reelection.
- ICE retained the detention-system `voluntary work` program paying detained people approximately $1/day.
- GEO has operated a large share of ICE detention capacity and won major new detention, transport, and monitoring business.
- David Venturella, a former GEO executive, became acting ICE director in 2026 after leading ICE's detention-contract division.

### Supported inference
A mature Trump detention system already combines:

```text
state custody
-> private detention revenue
-> captive population
-> ultra-low-paid detainee labor
-> revolving-door personnel
```

This does not by itself establish a future program placing homeless or disabled people into compulsory work camps. It does establish that `custody + private profit + extremely cheap captive labor` is not hypothetical within the broader enforcement ecology.

### Primary questions
- What work is performed, for whom, and under what refusal conditions?
- Are benefits, privileges, disciplinary consequences, or basic necessities tied to participation?
- Which private firms capture value from detainee labor?
- Do future homelessness/civil-commitment facilities adopt related labor models?

## 9. Threat-intent handling

```text
statement of harmful intent
-> specificity
-> means/capability
-> preparation
-> opportunity

-> target vulnerability
-> escalation indicators
-> countervailing constraints
```

Do not wait for completed harm before recognizing a credible trajectory.
