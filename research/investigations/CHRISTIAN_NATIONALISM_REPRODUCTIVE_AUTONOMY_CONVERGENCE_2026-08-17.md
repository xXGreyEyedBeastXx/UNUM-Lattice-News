# Christian Nationalism and Reproductive-Autonomy Convergence — 2026-08-17

Status: ACTIVE INVESTIGATION / PATTERN SUPPORTED / COORDINATION PARTIALLY UNRESOLVED

## Research question

Do pronatalism, anti-abortion law, punishment of abortion patients, barriers to care for pregnant minors, child-marriage / statutory-rape exception structures, and patriarchal gender-order politics form a recurring Christian-nationalist reproductive-control pattern even where states, organizations, and actors remain formally separate?

## Answer at current evidence seat

**Yes, a pattern-level convergence is supported.**

The evidence does **not** establish a single command center directing every relevant law, nor does it establish that every Christian nationalist, Christian, Republican, anti-abortion activist, child-marriage defender, or pronatalist holds every other position in this cluster.

The pattern is nevertheless stronger than coincidence or mere rhetorical similarity because multiple independent evidence families converge on the same direction of effect:

1. peer-reviewed research links Christian nationalism to nationalist pronatalism;
2. newer peer-reviewed research links Christian nationalism and replacement-theory belief specifically to selective pronatalism aimed at demographic dominance;
3. peer-reviewed research links Christian nationalism and White nationalism to support for punitive state action against women who obtain abortions;
4. current abortion bans in multiple states omit rape/incest exceptions;
5. Tennessee enacted barriers to third-party assistance for minors seeking abortion without parental/guardian consent and rejected a proposed under-13 exception from criminal abortion in committee;
6. U.S. child-marriage and statutory-rape law have historically intersected through marital exemptions that can legalize sexual conduct that otherwise would constitute statutory rape;
7. prior Lattice research preserves concrete examples in which lawmakers resisting complete child-marriage bans invoked fertility, pregnancy, or avoidance of abortion.

These edges establish **convergence**. Organizational coordination, common funding, model legislation, shared personnel, and actor-specific motive remain separate investigative questions.

Christian nationalism is seated here as a political-nationalist ideology concerning nation, public authority, law, and social order. It must not be projected onto Christians as a religious population.

## Mechanism 1 — demographic threat -> pronatalism

Perry et al. (2022) define nationalist pronatalism as promoting fertility to strengthen the nation and avoid national decline. In nationally representative U.S. data, Christian nationalism was the second-strongest predictor of nationalist pronatalism after patriarchal attitudes; perceived discrimination against Christians or Whites also predicted it.

Perry (2026) distinguishes general pronatalism from **selective pronatalism**: encouraging reproduction among selected populations to preserve demographic dominance. Christian nationalism and replacement-theory belief predicted selective pronatalism, while ordinary religious commitment predicted general pronatalism differently.

This makes demographic preference a necessary discriminator. Wanting families to have support is not selective pronatalism. Wanting a preferred ethno-religious population to reproduce in order to preserve dominance is.

## Mechanism 2 — gender order -> state control of fertility

Recent peer-reviewed research on complementarianism finds that beliefs assigning men and women distinct God-given roles are associated not only with private family hierarchy but with national pronatalism and preferences for state control over women's fertility, including abortion, birth control, and travel restrictions.

Complementarianism is not identical to Christian nationalism. It is a crossing ideology / gender-order mechanism that can conduct reproductive-control preferences into Christian-nationalist environments.

## Mechanism 3 — anti-abortion law -> bodily-autonomy constraint

The term **pro-life** records a movement's preferred moral frame. It does not neutralize the legal mechanism.

Where law prohibits abortion, the pregnant person's legal authority to decide whether the pregnancy continues is narrowed or removed. That is a bodily/reproductive-autonomy constraint regardless of whether supporters' stated motive is fetal protection, religion, demographic concern, or another rationale.

This does not mean fetal moral status is irrelevant to motive. It means motive and direction of legal effect are separate evidence classes.

As of April 1, 2026, KFF reports nine states with abortion bans or early gestational limits in effect had no rape or incest exception. Thus, in those jurisdictions, sexual violence does not itself create a legal abortion exception.

## Mechanism 4 — punishment / coercion

A 2025 peer-reviewed nationally representative study found Christian nationalism and White nationalism among the strongest predictors of support for arresting women who obtain abortions after anti-abortion sentiment itself. The strongest support occurred where the two ideologies overlapped.

The study also identifies an abortion-abolitionist submovement grounded in conservative Christian / Christian-nationalist teachings that explicitly seeks criminal accountability for women obtaining abortions rather than only providers.

This is a documented submovement. It must not be silently promoted into the position of every anti-abortion organization or every Christian nationalist.

## Mechanism 5 — pregnant minors and exit from abuse

Tennessee provides unusually legible state-level specimens:

- SB1971 / Public Chapter 1032 (2024) created an offense for adults who intentionally recruit, harbor, or transport an unemancipated pregnant minor for specified abortion-related purposes without required parental/guardian consent. Its legislative design states that the minor's consent is not a defense.
- HB2603 (2024) proposed exempting abortions performed on patients under 13 from Tennessee's criminal-abortion offense. It failed 2-5 in the Population Health Subcommittee.
- HB1440 (2023) proposed a rape/incest exception with law-enforcement reporting, contemplated forensic examination, gestational limits, and a mandatory minimum sentence for a false rape/incest report made to obtain an abortion.

These records support a concrete autonomy question: **what options remain available to a pregnant child when the pregnancy results from abuse, when a parent/guardian may be unsafe, or when care requires leaving the state?**

They do not establish that every sponsor intended to protect an abuser. The effect and the intent must remain separately typed.

## Mechanism 6 — child marriage and statutory-rape exceptions

Peer-reviewed U.S. research comparing marriage records and statutory-rape laws found that child marriages violated statutory-rape laws in 14 states during the studied period and that, in 33 states, some or all statutory-rape provisions exempted sex between married persons. The authors conclude that the coexistence of child marriage and marital exemptions can create loopholes for sexual acts with children that otherwise would be crimes.

This is stronger and more precise than a generalized claim that the movement is simply lowering the statutory age of consent nationwide.

The existing Lattice source bundle also preserves two motive-relevant specimens:

- Missouri lawmaker Hardy Billington argued that preventing a pregnant 17-year-old from marrying could increase the chance of abortion.
- New Hampshire Rep. Jess Edwards opposed a complete under-18 marriage ban while discussing 16- and 17-year-olds as being at a "ripe" and "fertile" age and arguing that marriage could reduce abortion incentives.

Those statements show **anti-abortion / fertility reasoning being used in defense of minor-marriage availability**. They do not prove an intent to facilitate rape.

## Pattern conclusion

The current evidence supports this transformation path:

```text
ethno-religious / national demographic threat
        -> nationalist or selective pronatalism
        -> patriarchal / complementarian reproductive role
        -> fetus or national reproduction prioritized over unilateral pregnancy termination
        -> abortion restriction and, in some subcultures, patient punishment
        -> rape/incest exceptions narrowed or absent
        -> parental / travel control over pregnant minors
        -> minor-marriage and marital-exemption structures can reduce statutory-rape protection
        -> reduced exit / care / autonomy for some women and children
```

No single edge proves every other edge. The chain is a **pattern model assembled from independently sourced mechanisms**.

## What would strengthen the movement-level causal chain

Investigate:

- shared advocacy organizations across abortion, pronatalism, child marriage, and parental-control campaigns;
- model legislation and legal drafting networks;
- donor / foundation overlap;
- church, think-tank, media, and political-party infrastructure;
- personnel who move between Christian-nationalist, white-nationalist, abortion-abolitionist, pronatalist, and family-policy organizations;
- explicit demographic / replacement rhetoric attached to specific state policy campaigns;
- policy documents that connect fertility, gender hierarchy, marriage age, abortion, and national continuity.

## Falsifiers / narrowing evidence

The pattern should be narrowed if:

- a purported Christian-nationalist campaign is better explained by a different ideology with no recoverable CN relation;
- a state or organization strengthens reproductive autonomy while otherwise matching the hypothesized network, requiring differentiation rather than averaging;
- alleged shared funding, personnel, or model-law transmission cannot be recovered after a defined search;
- an age-of-consent claim is actually only a marriage-age or marital-exemption issue and should be retyped;
- a claimed policy effect is contradicted by the enacted text or controlling court decision.
