# AIPAC Political, Social, and Business Capture System Case v0.1

Status: active investigation  
Legacy source branch: `organic-lattice-sorting-2026-08-11`

## Scope lock

This investigation concerns the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC), its PAC, United Democracy Project (UDP), identifiable donors, allied organizations, legislative and electoral influence, institutional relationships, policy/speech effects, beneficiary gains, and harms.

Do not collapse:

```text
AIPAC != Jewish people != Judaism != Zionism != Israel != Israeli government != every pro-Israel organization
```

Criticism of a lobby, ideology, government, donor network, or policy is not collective attribution to Jews.

## Core question

How does AIPAC and the broader pro-Israel political influence network convert money, access, endorsements, independent expenditures, lobbying, donor relationships, institutional alliances, and public narratives into durable political power in the United States; who gains political access, policy protection, institutional capacity, or insulation through those mechanisms; what harms and democratic constraints follow; and where does the claimed influence fail or have independent causal explanations?

## Legislative-policy lane

Track legislation that materially protects Israel, penalizes boycotts, conditions federal contracts/funding, or imports antisemitism definitions into enforcement.

Historical examples queued for detailed tracing:

- H.R. 3050, Countering Hate Against Israel by Federal Contractors Act: proposed federal-contract certification against boycotting Israel;
- H.R. 4795, Protect Economic and Academic Freedom Act of 2025: proposed higher-education funding consequences for certain commercial boycotts of Israel;
- H.R. 2446 / S. 1182, Stop Antisemitism on College Campuses Act: proposed funding consequences tied to IHRA antisemitism definition;
- H.R. 406, PROTECT Jewish Student and Faculty Act: proposed required campus antisemitism definition and sanctions;
- H.R. 7018, Stand with Israel Act of 2026: proposed U.S. funding consequences for UN bodies if Israel is expelled.

For each bill distinguish:

```text
sponsor/cosponsors
AIPAC position if documented
other supporting organizations
constitutional/civil-liberties concerns
antisemitism-protection rationale
Israel-policy effect
status
votes/outcomes
```

Legislative alignment is not automatically evidence of AIPAC causation.

A protection rationale belongs as attributable framing and as claim-specific counterevidence only where it changes the necessity, mechanism, scope, or rights-harm analysis. It is not a moral offset.

## Antisemitism / speech / campus lane

Investigate the use of IHRA antisemitism definitions and related enforcement in universities, immigration, federal funding, and employment.

Preserve both when they bear on the same mechanism:

- documented antisemitic harassment or violence against Jewish students/persons;
- documented or credibly alleged conflation of criticism of Israel/Zionism with antisemitism or suppression of protected speech.

The recovered investigation identifies H.R. 6806 (Antisemitism Response and Prevention Act of 2025) as useful internal critique because its findings allege weaponization of antisemitism accusations by the Trump administration while still acknowledging real antisemitism.

This is not positive balancing. It discriminates between actual antisemitic harm and use of antisemitism policy as a suppression mechanism.

## Social/institutional capture hypothesis

Working hypothesis, not conclusion:

```text
large donor base
 -> campaign finance capacity
 -> candidate selection / electoral pressure
 -> legislative access
 -> policy reinforcement
 -> institutional signaling
 -> donor/philanthropic pressure
 -> speech/norm enforcement
 -> lower political cost for pro-Israel policy
 -> higher political cost for dissent
```

Every arrow must be tested independently.

## Causal discriminators / falsifiers

These exist to test the capture/control hypothesis, not to build a favorable biography:

1. ordinary democratic interest-group influence within legal campaign-finance rules could explain some observed effects without institutional capture;
2. high-intensity single-issue voter/donor coordination could produce similar patterns without candidate control;
3. broader U.S. strategic or ideological support for Israel may persist independently of AIPAC;
4. Christian Zionist, defense, geopolitical, partisan, or donor networks may be coequal or stronger drivers;
5. AIPAC may amplify preferences produced elsewhere rather than originate them;
6. AIPAC-backed candidates may oppose AIPAC on meaningful policy questions;
7. candidates may defeat heavy AIPAC/UDP spending;
8. policy may fail despite AIPAC support;
9. major pro-Israel legislation may pass without documented AIPAC pressure;
10. documented Jewish organizations may oppose AIPAC, falsifying any attempted identity-level generalization;
11. an antisemitism-enforcement mechanism may protect Jewish people without suppressing Israel criticism in a scoped case;
12. AIPAC may diverge from a sitting Israeli government on a specific policy.

Only the discriminator relevant to the claim being tested belongs in that claim record. Do not accumulate these as positive credits.
