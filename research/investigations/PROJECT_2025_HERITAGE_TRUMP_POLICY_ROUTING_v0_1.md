# Project 2025 / Heritage / Trump Policy Routing v0.1

Status: active investigation  
Legacy source branch: `organic-lattice-sorting-2026-08-11`

## Purpose

Trace the relationship among the Heritage Foundation, Project 2025, former Trump personnel, Trump administrations, and implemented policy. Distinguish documented personnel/policy routing from unsupported claims of centralized control.

## Core locks

```text
Heritage Foundation != Trump administration
Project 2025 != every Trump policy
policy overlap != proof of direct instruction
personnel overlap != proof of total capture
documented routing != conspiracy inference
```

## Established structural links in the recovered investigation

The recovered research records Heritage as stating that the first Trump administration embraced nearly 64% of the 2016 Mandate for Leadership recommendations within its first year.

Project 2025 was explicitly organized around four implementation pillars:

1. policy agenda;
2. personnel recruitment/database;
3. administration training;
4. a 180-day agency implementation playbook.

The recovered investigation records multiple former Trump administration officials in Project 2025 roles, including Paul Dans, John McEntee, Troup Hemenway, Rick Dearborn, Russ Vought, Christopher Miller, Ken Cuccinelli, Peter Navarro, and others.

## Personnel-is-policy lane

Trace:

```text
Heritage / coalition organization
  -> Project 2025 role
  -> prior Trump administration role
  -> second Trump administration appointment
  -> agency authority
  -> policy implementation
```

Do not infer causation solely from overlap; identify exact recommendations, appointment authority, implemented action, and dates.

## Welfare / health / homelessness seam

The recovered investigation records Heritage advocacy for stronger work requirements across welfare programs, including work/community-engagement conditions for Medicaid, SNAP, TANF, and housing assistance for defined able-bodied populations.

It also records the second Trump administration as enacting or pursuing:

- Medicaid work/community-engagement requirements for some beneficiaries;
- tighter SNAP work rules, including changes affecting homeless individuals;
- increased criminalization/removal of street homelessness;
- expanded civil commitment/institutional placement policy;
- federal workforce restructuring increasing presidential removal authority.

These nodes should be tested for routing from Heritage/Project 2025 rather than assumed to be one unified program.

## Federal workforce / administrative-state lane

Project 2025 openly framed the administrative state as a primary obstacle and built a personnel/training strategy to make a future conservative administration more capable of rapid implementation.

The recovered investigation records the second Trump administration as pursuing Schedule Policy/Career-type reclassification for policy-influencing career positions, increasing at-will removal authority.

Test:

```text
Project 2025 recommendation
 -> Trump executive order / regulation
 -> affected positions
 -> due-process change
 -> oversight/capture consequences
```

## Competing causal models

These are causal discriminators, not positive balancing:

1. Heritage directly shaped implementation through personnel and policy preparation.
2. Heritage mostly codified priorities Trump and allied conservatives already shared.
3. Both Heritage and Trump emerged from the same broader conservative coalition, producing correlated policy without centralized command.
4. Personnel circulation functioned as the main transmission mechanism rather than formal policy adoption.
5. Some policies align only superficially and have independent origins.

## Accountability questions

- Who wrote the recommendation?
- Did they later hold government power?
- Was the policy adopted substantially or only rhetorically?
- What changed in law, regulation, funding, staffing, or enforcement?
- Who gained discretion, money, access, authority, or insulation?
- Who lost benefits, due process, autonomy, material security, or institutional protection?
- What claim-specific counterevidence shows nonimplementation, independent origin, modification, judicial blocking, or internal resistance?

## Current working hypothesis

Project 2025 should be modeled as an implementation infrastructure for a broader conservative governing coalition: policy development + personnel vetting + training + early-action planning. The Heritage–Trump relationship is therefore best tested as a routing network rather than a single-command hierarchy.

## Harm-audit locks

- A policy overlap is a lead until the recommendation-to-implementation path is sourced.
- Shared ideology is not itself proof of command.
- Nonimplementation or independent origin matters when it defeats a specific routing claim; it is not reputation credit.
- Unrelated Heritage or administration policy achievements do not offset harm traced through a confirmed routing path.
