1. What has Donald Trump been **adjudicated** to have done?
2. What has been **alleged by named people**, with what corroboration and procedural history?

> When Trump has more money, status, ownership, publicity, litigation capacity, or institutional leverage than the other party, how does he describe and use that asymmetry, and who absorbs the cost when the interaction fails?

That is a hypothesis to test edge-by-edge, not a master conclusion that substitutes for evidence.

## 1. The E. Jean Carroll anchor is not an allegation anymore

A federal civil jury found Trump liable for **sexual abuse** of E. Jean Carroll and for defaming her. The $5 million judgment was affirmed by the Second Circuit, rehearing was denied, and the Supreme Court declined review in 2026. A separate jury awarded Carroll $83.3 million for 2019 defamation; the Second Circuit affirmed that judgment, rehearing was denied in April 2026, and a Supreme Court certiorari petition remained pending as of 2026-08-27.

Important precision:

- this is a **civil adjudication**, not a criminal conviction;
- the 2023 jury did not select the New York statutory `rape` option on its verdict form;
- the district court and later appellate discussion explained that the factual finding supported digital penetration and that ordinary-language descriptions of the conduct may be broader than the statutory verdict label;
- therefore `not criminally convicted of rape` does **not** update the proposition `jury found no sexual abuse`—that proposition is false.

This is the central example of why **criminal non-adjudication cannot be used to erase civil adjudication**.

## 3. Trump's own recorded language matters at its own seat

### Access Hollywood

Trump was recorded in 2005 describing pursuing women, kissing them, grabbing them by the genitals, and saying celebrity status let him get away with it. After release he acknowledged the recording and apologized for the remarks, while characterizing them as locker-room talk.

What this updates:

- his expressed conception of celebrity power and sexual access;
- reliability of a blanket claim that he has never conceived of using status to cross sexual boundaries;
- pattern/context analysis where an allegation itself describes fame, ownership, or status as part of the mechanism.

What it does **not** do:

- automatically prove any named woman's allegation;
- establish the exact criminal elements of a specific incident.

### Pageant backstage statement

In a 2005 Howard Stern interview Trump said he could enter backstage while pageant contestants were dressing because he owned the pageant, described women standing without clothes, and said he could get away with things like that.

This is unusually relevant because separate pageant contestants later alleged unannounced dressing-room entry. The statement is direct evidence of an **ownership → access → reduced boundary** model. It still does not prove every contestant's memory or establish that his Stern statement referred specifically to Miss Teen USA.

### Power bridge

The narrow recurring self-authored relation is:

```text
status / celebrity / ownership
-> privileged access
-> expectation of reduced resistance or reduced accountability
-> “get away with it” language
```

That relation can be compared to allegations without laundering allegation into fact.

## 4. Business counterparties show a parallel leverage structure

Trump-linked companies filed **six Chapter 11 corporate bankruptcies**. Trump has never filed personal bankruptcy.

The six commonly counted filings are:

1. Trump Taj Mahal — 1991
2. Trump Castle — 1992
3. Trump Plaza casino — 1992
4. Plaza Hotel, New York — 1992
5. Trump Hotels & Casino Resorts — 2004
6. Trump Entertainment Resorts — 2009

The first four followed severe overleveraging and cash-flow collapse. The Taj was financed with roughly $675 million in high-interest junk bonds after cheaper financing could not be secured; the broader Trump empire was carrying billions in debt. The later public casino companies continued to carry large debt burdens; the 2009 filing also occurred amid the post-2008 recession/casino downturn.

`Used Chapter 11 lawfully` is a valid statement. It is not a rebuttal to:

- whether leverage was reckless;
- whether creditors, contractors, shareholders, and vendors absorbed losses;
- whether Trump preserved disproportionate personal value while shifting loss outward;
- whether public claims about business success accurately describe those outcomes.

## 5. Specific counterparties and harmed populations

### Taj contractors and vendors

Contemporaneous reporting documented tens of millions of dollars in unpaid Taj Mahal bills shortly after opening, mostly owed to contractors. Some small firms were reported near bankruptcy. Contractors negotiated, sued, and in some cases accepted less than amounts claimed due as the Trump casino structure moved toward bankruptcy.

This is not merely hindsight after a failed casino. The unpaid-bill crisis was publicly documented **before** the 1991 bankruptcy.

### Trump Tower Polish demolition workers

Court records document roughly 200 undocumented Polish workers employed by the demolition contractor on the future Trump Tower site, lower pay than union labor, off-the-books employment, and unpaid benefit-fund contributions. A Department of Labor action produced a judgment against the demolition contractor for unpaid wages. Litigation involving Trump-related entities continued for years and was eventually settled.

Trump's exact personal knowledge remains a separately contested edge; the exploitative labor structure itself is documented.

### Public casino shareholders

Trump Hotels and Casino Resorts raised about $140 million from public investors in 1995. The company lost money repeatedly and eventually became a penny stock and entered bankruptcy. Financial filings and reporting show Trump nonetheless received substantial salary, bonuses, fees, related-party benefits, and company-paid expenses during the period.

That establishes the relation:

```text
company/shareholder loss
coexisting with
Trump personal compensation/benefit
```

It does not by itself establish criminal self-dealing.

### Trump University

Fraud litigation brought by students and the New York attorney general ended in a **$25 million settlement** benefiting former students/clients. Because it settled, this is not a trial verdict finding every allegation true. It is materially different from saying there was never any legal consequence.

### Trump Foundation

This is stronger. Trump agreed to **19 factual admissions** concerning misuse of Trump Foundation assets; a New York court ordered a $2 million payment, and the foundation was dissolved under court supervision. This is not an unresolved allegation.

### New York business fraud case

A New York trial court found repeated/persistent fraud in Trump financial statements. In 2025 the appellate division vacated the enormous disgorgement penalty while the fraud/liability findings were not simply erased; the fractured appellate opinions left further review available. Therefore `the fine was thrown out` is not equivalent to `the fraud finding was reversed as false`.

## 6. The recurring defense error

A common defense pattern is:

```text
not criminally convicted
-> therefore did not happen
```

That is invalid. The correct discriminator is:

```text
criminal conviction?
civil adjudication?
administrative finding?
settlement with factual admissions?
settlement without admissions?
sworn allegation?
contemporaneous corroboration?
direct recording?
withdrawal/dismissal on merits or procedure?
no case filed at all?
```

These evidence states have different weights, but **none can be replaced with the single word “unadjudicated.”**

Likewise:

```text
not adjudicated != disproven
settled != admitted unless terms say so
withdrawn != false
civil liability != criminal conviction
criminal non-prosecution != innocence finding
successful appeal on remedy != erasure of underlying factual/legal finding
```

## 7. Current synthesis

The evidence presently supports a narrower but substantial pattern proposition:

> Across sexual-boundary allegations and business-counterparty disputes, there is repeated evidence of interactions in which Trump possessed much greater status, ownership, money, litigation capacity, or institutional leverage than the other party. His own recorded statements sometimes describe that asymmetry as permitting conduct or advantage that ordinary people could not obtain. Some resulting harms are adjudicated, some settled with admissions, some documented through financial/court records, and many sexual allegations remain unresolved person-specific claims.

That proposition does **not** require pretending every allegation is true. It also cannot be defeated by pointing out that every event did not produce a criminal conviction.

## Tiny locks

> Protection from accountability is not exoneration.

> Absence of adjudication is an evidence-state fact, not a factual finding that the conduct did not occur.

> One adjudicated assault does not prove every allegation. One unresolved allegation does not erase the adjudicated assault.

> Power is not proof. But when power is part of the alleged mechanism and the actor independently describes using power to obtain access or impunity, that evidence belongs in the model.
