# Gaza Border / IDF Interaction in the Year Before October 7 — 2022-2023

Status: ACTIVE INVESTIGATION / MANAGED-INSTABILITY PATTERN SUPPORTED / DETERRENCE MODEL CONTRADICTED BY OBSERVED CLUES

## Question

What actually happened between Gaza, Hamas/other armed groups, and Israeli border/security forces during roughly the year before October 7, 2023?

## Core finding

The year was not a period of simple quiet followed by an unforeseeable rupture. It was a period of **managed instability**: recurrent rocket-fire/airstrike exchanges, visible Hamas training, Gaza-border confrontations, work-permit openings/closures used as a security lever, and repeated Israeli assessments that Hamas wanted quiet and had been deterred.

The important contradiction is that this calming/deterrence model strengthened while contrary clues accumulated.

## Timeline

### Late 2022

Post-Oct. 7 IDF investigations later concluded that Hamas had decided in April 2022 to pursue a large-scale attack and was approximately 85% ready by September 2022. Israeli intelligence also possessed a detailed large-ground-invasion plan in 2022 but treated it as aspirational/unrealistic.

On 28 December 2022 Hamas's annual `Strong Pillar` exercise publicly simulated an assault on an Israeli military base and seizure of soldier hostages. AP later geolocated one training site near Beit Hanoun to roughly 800 meters from the Israeli border.

Important posture:

`POST_HOC_IDF_FINDING_OF_HAMAS_READINESS != PROOF_ISRAEL_KNEW_HAMAS_INTERNAL_EXECUTION_DATE_AT_THE_TIME`

but

`ISRAELI_POSSESSION_OF_LARGE_INVASION_PLAN = SUPPORTED`.

### January 26-27, 2023

After a deadly Israeli raid in Jenin, Gaza militants fired rockets toward Israel; Israel responded with airstrikes, including against a Hamas training site.

This illustrates that Gaza and the West Bank were already coupled operational theaters: West Bank events could produce Gaza-border escalation.

### February 2023

After an Israeli raid in Nablus killed 11 Palestinians, militants in Gaza fired rockets and Israel conducted airstrikes in Gaza.

The exchange was interpreted publicly as limited and contained rather than a precursor to broader war.

### April 5-7, 2023

Israeli police entered Al-Aqsa Mosque during Ramadan/Passover tensions. Rockets were fired from Gaza, and a major barrage came from southern Lebanon. Israel struck Gaza and Lebanon, attributing Hamas responsibility for relevant attacks/targets.

Again, Jerusalem/West Bank tensions and Gaza-border escalation were causally coupled.

### May 2-13, 2023 — Operation Shield and Arrow

More than 100 rockets were fired from Gaza on May 2 after the death of Palestinian Islamic Jihad figure Khader Adnan. On May 9 Israel launched `Operation Shield and Arrow`, killing senior Palestinian Islamic Jihad commanders and striking PIJ infrastructure.

The IDF states that 1,469 rockets were launched during the five-day conflict.

Hamas notably did not join the fighting directly.

This became a major interpretive clue: later IDF investigations specifically identified Hamas's decision to remain outside rounds of fighting with PIJ as one of the observations Israel misread under its prevailing conception of Hamas.

Instead of reading restraint as potentially strategic concealment, senior Israeli officials increasingly read it as proof that Hamas wanted quiet and was deterred.

### June 14, 2023 — concessions / quiet model

Later Israeli reporting on internal security discussions states that senior officials considered expanding Gaza economic concessions, including labor-entry visas and infrastructure measures.

Reported statements included the view that Hamas had no interest in violence in Gaza, wanted quiet, and could be further restrained through economic incentives.

Correct posture:

`HAMAS_NONPARTICIPATION_IN_ONE_ROUND != ABSENCE_OF_LARGE_ATTACK_INTENT`.

### Summer 2023

The border remained under a high-technology defense model: the approximately $1.1 billion barrier completed in 2021 relied heavily on sensors, cameras, remote systems and fortified infrastructure. AP later documented that some manned functions had been replaced by remote systems.

This technology-centric posture was paired with confidence that a major ground assault would either be prevented or detected with enough warning to respond.

### September 12, 2023 — the central contradiction

On September 12 Hamas publicly released a live-fire `Strong Pillar` rehearsal showing fighters:

- blasting through a replica border gate;
- entering in pickup trucks;
- destroying mock border towers/communications infrastructure;
- moving building-to-building through a mock Israeli locality;
- firing at human-shaped targets.

AP verified the exercise and geolocated multiple training areas from Hamas-released material; some were close enough to the border to be observable from Israeli positions.

Later Israeli reporting says that on the **same date**, IDF Chief of Staff Herzi Halevi toured the Gaza border and assessed that `Operation Shield and Arrow` had succeeded in deterring Hamas and that the border communities were secure from a Hamas invasion.

This pairing is a high-value clue:

`VISIBLE_ASSAULT_REHEARSAL + PUBLIC_DETERRENCE_CONFIDENCE`.

### Mid-to-late September 2023 — border confrontations resume

For roughly two weeks Palestinians held repeated demonstrations along the Gaza fence. Participants threw rocks and explosive devices; incendiary balloons ignited fires in Israeli farmland. Israeli troops used force at the fence, Palestinians were killed/wounded, and Israel carried out airstrikes on Hamas posts.

Israel closed the Erez crossing, suspending access for approximately 18,000 Gazan workers, then reopened it on September 28 after mediation and reduced tensions.

The permit system was explicitly treated as a mechanism for maintaining calm: work access could be opened or closed in response to security conditions.

The immediate pre-October border therefore was **not quiet in the ordinary sense**. It had just experienced days of explosions, fence confrontations, Israeli airstrikes and crossing closure.

### October 2023 approach

Despite the preceding events:

- the dominant Israeli concept still treated Hamas as deterred / preferring economic stability;
- large-attack plans had been dismissed as beyond capability;
- visible training had been normalized;
- border unrest had been interpreted as manageable bargaining/coercion rather than preparation for invasion;
- economic concessions/work permits were restored;
- a last-minute large civilian festival near the Gaza border was approved despite local troop-availability objections;
- reinforcement forces were moved toward West Bank security demands;
- short-term warning indicators on October 6-7 were still interpreted below the threshold of a mass invasion.

## Pattern

```text
REPEATED LIMITED ESCALATION
-> ISRAEL RESPONDS WITH AIRSTRIKES / BORDER CONTROL / ECONOMIC LEVERS
-> HAMAS SOMETIMES RESTRAINS DIRECT PARTICIPATION
-> RESTRAINT READ AS DETERRENCE / PRAGMATISM
-> ECONOMIC INCENTIVES EXPANDED
-> VISIBLE TRAINING NORMALIZED
-> HIGH-TECH BARRIER TRUSTED
-> CONTRARY WARNINGS ASSIMILATED INTO SAME MODEL
-> FORCE POSTURE REMAINS INADEQUATE FOR MASS GROUND INCURSION
```

This is better described as `MODEL_PROTECTIVE_NORMALIZATION` than absence of warning.

## Relevance to Nova

The Nova approval should therefore not be evaluated against a fictional baseline of a calm border with no recent warning behavior.

The relevant context was:

- recurrent Gaza-Israel fire during the year;
- a five-day war in May;
- Hamas's strategic nonparticipation being read as deterrence;
- public invasion-style drills;
- renewed fence violence and Israeli strikes in September;
- Erez closure/reopening days before the attack;
- a security doctrine confident in economic containment and the barrier;
- local force-availability concerns;
- preexisting intelligence on a large Hamas ground-invasion concept.

The open question is not whether there were warning clues. There were.

The open question is **why decision-makers repeatedly interpreted those clues through a model that kept producing reassurance and under-protection.**

## Locks

- Recurrent border violence != proof an October 7-scale attack was certain.
- Hamas training in public != proof Israel knew the exact attack date.
- Work permits/economic concessions != responsibility for Hamas's attack.
- Hamas restraint in May 2023 != evidence of peaceful intent.
- Israeli airstrikes/closures != proof every Gaza escalation was initiated by one side.
- West Bank/Jerusalem triggers repeatedly producing Gaza responses means the theaters were operationally coupled.
- A security model can fail because contrary evidence is normalized rather than absent.
- `CALM_AS_INTERPRETED != LOW_OBJECTIVE_RISK`.
