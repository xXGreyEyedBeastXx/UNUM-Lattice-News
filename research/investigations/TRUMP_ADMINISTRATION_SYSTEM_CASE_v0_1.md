## Purpose

This file treats the Trump administration as a system-level investigative object without presupposing a verdict. Individual scandals and actor dossiers are subordinate evidence trees. The central question is whether recurring observed outcomes are better explained by legitimate policy objectives and ordinary political patronage, or by a persistent architecture of private enrichment, preferential access, institutional capture, coercion, retaliation, and protection.

Every major mechanism should carry three seats:

1. **Case against / harm model** — strongest evidence of corruption, self-enrichment, abuse, capture, coercion, or avoidable harm.
2. **Case for / legitimate-governance model** — strongest plausible public-interest rationale, beneficial outcomes, exculpatory evidence, and counterexamples.
3. **Discriminators** — observations that would cause one model to gain or lose weight.

Do not convert shared nodes into direct edges. Do not convert allegations into findings. Do not convert legal non-liability into factual exoneration. Do not erase beneficial outcomes because an actor is otherwise harmful.

## Probe A — Presidential information -> private monetization -> market advantage

### Observed structure

Trump Media & Technology Group announced Truth API in July 2026 as a licensed B2B feed providing real-time access to posts from high-ranking Truth Social accounts. TMTG explicitly described the product as a new recurring revenue stream and said institutional customers had already signed up before launch.

Associated Press subsequently reported that customers include high-frequency trading firms paying approximately $60,000-$100,000 per month for earlier/faster access to influential Truth Social content. Presidential communications can be market-moving.

```text
Donald Trump / presidential communication
        |
        v
Truth Social / Trump Media
        |
        +--> ordinary public distribution
        |
        +--> paid low-latency institutional feed
                    |
                    v
             financial traders
                    |
                    v
            possible market advantage

Trump Media <--- subscription revenue
```

### Harm/conflict model

A president can generate information through public office whose market significance is partly produced by presidential authority. A privately affiliated company can monetize faster access to that information. This creates a structural conflict even without proof that any individual post was deliberately timed or written to enrich the company or subscribers.

The strongest form of the allegation — deliberate market manipulation or intentional preferential disclosure — is **not established** by the existence of the API.

### Legitimate/business model

Truth Social is a private platform. Financial data vendors routinely sell low-latency feeds, and institutional customers routinely pay for speed. The product may be ordinary commercialization of platform data rather than corrupt use of presidential power. Trump Media has substantial operating losses, making development of recurring revenue commercially rational.

### Discriminators

- Does the paid feed receive Trump posts before the ordinary public can access them, and by how much?
- Are presidential policy announcements distributed through the paid pathway before equivalent official-government publication?
- Have subscribers demonstrably traded on the latency advantage?
- Are there internal policies separating official presidential announcements from monetized platform distribution?
- Does Trump retain financial benefit from TMTG while serving as president, directly or through trusts/holdings?
- Are comparable feeds available from other platforms on comparable terms?
- Did government action materially privilege Truth Social/TMTG relative to competitors?

**Current weight:** documented conflict architecture; corrupt intent/unlawful manipulation unproven.

## Probe B — Family crypto -> public policy -> private wealth

### Observed structure

Reuters reported from Trump's 2025 financial disclosure that Trump received more than $1.4 billion in crypto-related income, including World Liberty Financial token activity and meme-coin income. Reuters separately estimated the Trump family's crypto-related earnings since his return to office at more than $2.3 billion.

During the same administration, federal policy has become materially more crypto-friendly. The SEC and CFTC issued a March 2026 interpretation clarifying federal securities-law treatment of crypto assets, while the administration and congressional allies have pursued broader digital-asset legislation. Reuters reports the crypto industry has spent heavily in support of favorable legislation.

Reuters also reported that Trump and his sons publicly promoted crypto while Trump's disclosed conventional stocks and bonds grew substantially as crypto-derived wealth was converted/diversified into traditional assets.

```text
presidential administration
      |
      +--> crypto policy / regulatory posture
      |               |
      |               v
      |        sector opportunity/value
      |               |
      v               v
public promotion -> Trump-family crypto ventures
                         |
                         v
                    private income
                         |
                         v
              diversified conventional wealth
```

### Harm/conflict model

The president and immediate family possess unusually large direct financial exposure to an industry whose regulatory environment is being changed by the president's administration. Even when individual regulatory decisions have legitimate policy rationales, this creates a persistent conflict between public authority and private financial benefit.

### Legitimate-policy model

Crypto policy can be defended independently of Trump-family interests: regulatory clarity may reduce uncertainty, promote innovation, prevent firms from moving offshore, and create coherent jurisdiction between securities and commodities regulators. Congressional action is not controlled by Trump alone, and industry-wide benefits do not establish that policy was adopted for Trump's private gain.

### Discriminators

- Construct date-level series of Trump-family token sales/income against executive, SEC, CFTC, Treasury, banking, and legislative actions.
- Identify decisions uniquely or disproportionately beneficial to Trump-linked assets rather than crypto generally.
- Trace buyers/investors in Trump-linked crypto for foreign-government, regulated-industry, donor, contractor, or pardon-seeking relationships.
- Compare administration treatment of Trump-linked ventures with similarly situated competitors.
- Determine whether ethics screens, recusals, divestment, blind-trust structures, or other conflict controls exist and function.
- Identify policy actions adverse to Trump-family crypto interests; these are important counterevidence.

**Current weight:** very strong structural conflict; quid-pro-quo or policy-for-profit causation requires transaction-level evidence.

## Probe C — Tariffs/trade disruption -> farm distress -> government relief -> political credit

### Primary-source anchors

USDA announced $12 billion in Farmer Bridge Assistance for 2026. USDA's February 2026 description explicitly says $11 billion in one-time row-crop bridge payments respond to "temporary trade market disruptions and increased production costs." USDA attributes those conditions principally to prior Biden-era policy and unfair foreign trade practices.

USDA Economic Research Service forecasts direct government farm payments of $44.3 billion in 2026, up $13.8 billion / 45.2% from 2025. ERS says the increase reflects higher commodity-linked payments while supplemental and ad hoc disaster assistance remain high. Supplemental/ad hoc assistance is forecast at $23.9 billion, primarily including Farmer Bridge Assistance and disaster programs.

USDA also expanded ARC/PLC payment limits and eligibility in 2026 and announced $1.625 billion in specialty-crop assistance tied to elevated input costs and market disruption attributed to unfair foreign competition.

### Candidate feedback loop

```text
trade/tariff strategy
       |
       v
foreign retaliation + market reordering + input effects
       |
       v
farm revenue/cost pressure
       |
       v
federal bridge / commodity / disaster payments
       |
       v
short-term farm stabilization
       |
       +--> administration political credit
       |
       +--> fiscal/socialized cost
```

### Harm/self-reinforcing model

If administration tariff actions materially contribute to lost export markets, retaliatory tariffs, input-cost increases, or commodity-price pressure, subsequent federal relief can partially socialize the cost of the original policy while allowing the administration to claim credit for rescuing affected producers. This would be a policy-generated-harm -> relief -> political-credit loop.

### Legitimate-strategy model

Short-term disruption may be an anticipated cost of renegotiating structurally unfavorable trade relationships. Relief can rationally preserve domestic productive capacity during bargaining. Long-run gains could include diversified export markets, improved terms, domestic manufacturing, strategic independence, tariff revenue, or reduced vulnerability to foreign coercion. Some farm weakness also predates current tariffs and reflects commodity cycles, weather, interest rates, input costs, and earlier policy.

### Discriminators

- Commodity-by-commodity export volume/value before and after tariff actions.
- Retaliatory tariff exposure by commodity and geography.
- Input-cost changes attributable to tariffs versus other causes.
- Farm bankruptcies, delinquencies, land values, net cash income, and government-payment dependence by region.
- Distribution of relief: small/family farms versus large entities/pass-through structures.
- New durable export-market access actually obtained after bargaining.
- Net fiscal calculation: tariff revenue versus relief, lost output, higher procurement costs, and consumer/input effects.
- Counterfactual comparison with commodities/regions less exposed to tariff retaliation.

**Current weight:** documented large relief response and documented trade-market disruption; attribution of the full distress cycle to Trump tariffs remains an empirical question and must not be assumed.

## Probe D — Clemency -> network proximity / protection

DOJ's public 2025-present clemency ledger provides a primary-source population of grants. It includes broad January 6 clemency and individual pardons covering offenses including securities fraud/insider trading, campaign-finance violations, fraud, environmental offenses, drug offenses, and other federal crimes. DOJ also publishes denials, creating a potential comparison population.

### Investigative model

Do not cherry-pick famous pardons. Build the entire grant population and compare it against denials where data permits.

For each recipient, type edges to:

```text
Trump campaign / PAC donations
Trump family/business payments
Mar-a-Lago / club relationships
lobbyists / paid clemency advocates
Trump political work
media advocacy
crypto purchases/investment
foreign-government relationships
administration donors
ideological organizations
personal/family relationships
post-clemency financial or political benefit
```

### Harm/protection model

If clemency is disproportionately associated with money, personal access, political loyalty, ideological usefulness, or network proximity compared with similarly situated denied applicants, it would support a patronage/protection interpretation.

### Legitimate-clemency model

The pardon power is constitutionally broad. Presidents historically use it for mercy, perceived prosecutorial overreach, old convictions, political offenses, sentencing disparities, and cases promoted by advocates. Network proximity may partly reflect who can successfully bring deserving cases to presidential attention rather than corrupt exchange.

### Discriminators

- Compare grants and denials by offense, sentence, time served, restitution, age of conviction, and evidence of rehabilitation.
- Quantify donor/access/advocacy edges rather than relying on anecdotes.
- Identify grants with no Trump-network connection as counterexamples.
- Identify strongly connected applicants who were denied as counterexamples.
- Trace paid clemency lobbying and payments before/after grants.
- Separate categorical policy pardons from individualized discretionary pardons.

**Current weight:** primary population identified; network analysis not yet complete.

## Cross-probe topology emerging

The first pass reveals a recurring boundary problem rather than one proven master conspiracy:

```text
PUBLIC AUTHORITY
      |
      +--> information
      +--> regulation
      +--> trade policy
      +--> relief spending
      +--> clemency
      |
      v
BOUNDARY / ROUTER
      |
      +--> family businesses
      +--> private platform
      +--> industry networks
      +--> political allies
      +--> affected constituencies
      |
      v
PRIVATE / POLITICAL BENEFIT
```

The system-level investigative question is whether the boundary between public authority and private/political benefit is merely porous in the ordinary way of American politics or is systematically being engineered to route state power toward the president's family, allies, donors, ideological coalition, and commercial ecosystem.

This question must be answered by edge density, chronology, differential treatment, counterexamples, and material outcomes — not by rhetorical similarity.

## New high-value holes

1. **Foreign capital -> Trump-family assets -> U.S. policy.** Build a buyer/investor map for Trump-linked crypto, real estate, licensing, clubs, and other ventures; cross against foreign-policy events.
2. **DHS/immigration -> contractors/donors.** Trace detention, surveillance, transportation, construction, technology, and private-security contracts against political/network ties and human consequences.
3. **Advertising/government communications -> favored media/platforms.** Compare federal advertising and communications spending across outlets, including any shifts toward politically aligned platforms.
4. **Regulator -> former employer/industry.** Build appointment-level conflict map with recusals, holdings, former clients/employers, and regulatory outcomes.
5. **Children as boundary routers.** Eric Trump, Donald Trump Jr., and other family members should be modeled not merely as relatives but as independent commercial/political actors linking foreign capital, crypto, media, investment, and presidential access.
6. **Clemency comparison population.** Convert DOJ grants and denials into a structured dataset before making systemic claims.
7. **Farm distributional effects.** Determine who receives relief and who absorbs tariff/trade costs; distinguish family farms from large agribusiness and pass-through entities.
8. **Policy adverse to insiders.** Actively search for decisions that cost Trump, his family, donors, or allies money/power. These are necessary falsifiers for a capture model.
9. **Institutional resistance.** Record courts, inspectors, career officials, legislators, agencies, media, and private actors that successfully constrain administration behavior; do not model the state as monolithic.
10. **Cross-administration baseline.** Compare identified mechanisms with Biden, Obama, Bush, and earlier administrations where possible. A mechanism common to presidents may indict the wider institutional ecology rather than uniquely Trump.

## Method lock

This system case is not a declaration that Donald Trump or his administration constitutes a criminal enterprise, dictatorship, conspiracy, or corrupt administration. Those are conclusions requiring defined criteria and evidence.

Likewise, electoral victory, legal authority, absence of prosecution, or institutional approval does not establish that conduct is ethical, beneficial, truthful, or free of corruption.

The lattice should be capable of returning any of the following outcomes without embarrassment:

- allegation supported;
- allegation weakened;
- allegation disproven;
- conduct legal but structurally conflicted;
- conduct harmful but legitimately motivated;
- conduct beneficial despite suspect motives;
- ordinary systemic American corruption rather than Trump-specific behavior;
- Trump-specific intensification of an older system;
- insufficient evidence.

The objective is not to make Trump look bad or good. The objective is to make the causal topology difficult to lie about.
