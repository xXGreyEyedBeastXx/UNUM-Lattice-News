## CONFIRMED — Miller set an aggressive 3,000-arrests-per-day target

Reuters reported that Miller set an aggressive quota of **3,000 immigration arrests per day** in late May 2025. Reuters reported that efforts to meet the goal pushed immigration officers into more communities and businesses, triggered protests, and were associated with widened enforcement beyond the administration's public crime-focused framing.

Sources:
- Reuters profile, 2025-07-11: https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/the-white-house-aide-driving-trumps-aggressive-immigration-agenda-4131605

Confirmed topology:

```text
Miller policy pressure / numeric target
-> ICE leadership operational pressure
-> higher arrest-volume requirement
-> broader enforcement surface
-> increased detention / removal demand
```

Do not claim that every arrest resulting during this period was personally ordered by Miller. The confirmed contribution is the quota/intensification layer.

## CONFIRMED — quota pressure reached people outside serious-crime targeting

Reuters found ICE cast a wider net than under the Biden administration and increasingly arrested people without criminal convictions while the administration continued to frame the campaign around dangerous criminals. Reuters specifically tied the late-May escalation pressure to Miller's 3,000-per-day target.

Harm lanes:
- confinement/coercion;
- due-process exposure;
- family/community disruption;
- expanded state-police presence;
- increased demand for detention contractors and data systems.

Responsibility posture:
- candidate `R4` for the quota/intensification decision if final source reconstruction confirms his decisive policy authority;
- downstream individual arrests require event-specific attribution.

## PATH_FORWARD

- arrest population before/after the quota by conviction/charge/status category;
- detention-growth linkage to the quota period;
- field directives concerning courthouses, workplaces, farms, restaurants, hotels, schools, hospitals, and check-ins;
- internal objections, waivers, reversals, or evidence of who could overrule Miller;
- state/local deployments and use of military or National Guard capacity traceable to quota-driven unrest.

## CONFIRMED — OMB became a high-power spending gate

GAO documented multiple 2025 disputes in which congressionally appropriated funds were withheld, delayed, or subjected to rescission/apportionment processes. GAO also reported that OMB removed public apportionment data and in some reviews declined to provide updated apportionment schedules needed for oversight.

Sources:
- GAO B-337581, 2025-06-17: https://www.gao.gov/products/b-337581
- GAO B-337203, NIH, 2025: https://www.gao.gov/products/b-337203

- GAO B-337805, 2025-09-12: https://www.gao.gov/products/b-337805

GAO concluded in specific cases that executive agencies violated the Impoundment Control Act by withholding appropriated funds. Do not automatically attribute every agency-level violation personally to Vought without decision-specific documentation; the structural OMB chokepoint is confirmed.

## CONFIRMED — CFPB operational shutdown while Vought served as acting director

Reuters reported in February 2025 that while serving as acting CFPB director, Vought ordered a halt to nearly all bureau work, with most staff placed on administrative leave and significant firings underway. Litigation temporarily constrained further firings and required preservation of data and operating funds.

Source:
- Reuters, 2025-02-25: https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-admin-denies-plans-shutter-cfpb-court-papers-say-2025-02-25/

Harm lanes requiring contribution-level tracing:
- reduced consumer-protection enforcement;
- reduced complaint/investigation capacity;
- worker displacement;
- executive concentration over an agency created by statute.

Do not count hypothetical consumer losses until linked to enforcement cessation or abandoned cases.

## CONFIRMED — OMB policy explicitly prioritized military/homeland-security expansion while cutting other functions

The White House FY2026 budget release quotes Vought describing an agenda to end programs the administration characterized as ideological while delivering "unprecedented support" to military and homeland security priorities.

Source:
- White House OMB budget release, 2025-05: https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/05/the-white-house-office-of-management-and-budget-releases-the-presidents-fiscal-year-2026-skinny-budget/

This is confirmed as budget priority and rhetoric; downstream deprivation must be traced program by program.

## CONFIRMED counterevidence relevant to conflict scope — recusal/divestment

Source:
- Wall Street Journal reporting, August 2026, acting ICE chief divestment/recusal.

## CONFIRMED — Venturella narrowed post-release death reporting

ICE Directive 11003.7, dated June 2, 2026, removed deaths occurring within 30 days after release from the agency's notification/review/reporting scope. Washington Post reporting identified Venturella's memo as the operative direction.

Sources:
- AILA document archive / ICE Directive 11003.7: https://www.aila.org/library/ice-revises-policy-on-notification-review-and-reporting-of-deaths-in-detention-to-remove-post-release-deaths
- Washington Post, 2026-06-04: https://www.washingtonpost.com/immigration/2026/06/04/ice-stop-reporting-deaths-newly-released-detainees-internal-memo-says/

Confirmed harm lane:
- democratic/epistemic harm through reduced mortality visibility and weaker post-release accountability surface.

Do not infer that changing reporting caused a death.

## CONFIRMED — the reporting reduction has already changed what the public receives about a death

Reuters reported on August 13, 2026 that Jose Chajon-Raxon experienced seizure-like activity after detention at GEO-operated Delaney Hall, was hospitalized, released from ICE custody days later, and subsequently died. DHS said ICE had not been notified because it was not responsible for someone who died weeks after release. The administration did not disclose his death through the prior 30-day reporting framework because that framework had been removed.

Source:
- Reuters, 2026-08-13: https://www.reuters.com/world/us/immigrant-who-was-detained-ice-facility-new-jersey-died-after-medical-emergency-2026-08-13/

Scoped confirmation:

```text
Venturella-era directive removes post-release deaths from required ICE death reporting
+
Chajon-Raxon dies after release following detention medical emergency
-> death falls outside new ICE reporting regime
```

PENDING:
- whether ICE/GEO medical treatment caused or contributed to Chajon-Raxon's death;
- whether release timing was influenced by mortality-reporting incentives;
- any individual negligence or intentional concealment.

Those require medical, facility, release, and investigative records.
