## Working conclusion

The strongest current model is **not** a single secret command structure and **not** a set of unrelated conservative organizations that merely happen to resemble one another.

The evidence supports a **federated conservative power ecology** built over decades through partially overlapping institutional rails:

```text
corporate litigation
knowledge / academic production
legal-professional formation
think-tank and policy blueprint production
state-policy replication
philanthropic / donor capacity
judicial-selection and confirmation infrastructure
movement media / cultural organizations
personnel recruitment and training
executive-transition machinery
present government implementation
```

These rails contain real cooperation, shared funders, shared personnel, formal coalitions, and direct implementation edges. They also contain factional disagreement and institutional independence.

Therefore:

```text
coordination != total command
independence != absence of network
funding != command
personnel crossing != command
formal coalition membership = real coordination at the stated project seat
historical lineage != sole causation
factional fracture != absence of prior cooperation
```

## 1. Powell is a strategy crystallization inside a broader counter-mobilization

Lewis Powell's 1971 memo to the U.S. Chamber of Commerce is best seated as a **primary strategy document** inside an already-developing corporate and conservative reaction to the social, regulatory, academic, media, and political changes of the 1960s and early 1970s.

The memo explicitly joined domains that are often studied separately:

```text
business threat perception
-> coordinated institutional response
-> universities / scholars / textbooks
-> media monitoring and response
-> political power
-> legal staffing
-> strategic litigation
-> courts as instruments of change
```

The key correction to older caution is that at least one downstream relation is direct rather than merely analogous:

```text
Powell recommendation
-> U.S. Chamber
-> National Chamber Litigation Center created in 1977
```

The Chamber itself identifies the NCLC as having been launched on Powell's recommendation.

This establishes a `DIRECT_DOCUMENTED_IMPLEMENTATION` edge.

It does **not** establish that every later conservative legal institution descended from Powell personally.

## 2. 1973 is an unusually dense institutional year

## 3. Knowledge production becomes infrastructure

The John M. Olin Foundation provides a particularly clear material-flow node.

University of Chicago Law School records that Olin shifted substantial grantmaking toward preserving free-market institutions beginning around 1968 and funded law-and-economics programs and professorships across major law schools.

This creates a researchable rail:

```text
philanthropic capital
-> academic program / chair / center
-> scholarship / professional formation
-> legal doctrine / administrative practice / judicial interpretation ?
```

The first two edges can be documented relatively directly.

The later effects require citation, personnel, curriculum, clerkship, litigation, or judicial-opinion transmission evidence.

Do not infer ideological control over every scholar funded by an institution.

## 4. Heritage helps organize a conservative legal field

Heritage's own Edwin Meese history is unusually explicit.

Meese describes Heritage as supporting and helping organize conservative public-interest legal organizations that emerged beginning with Pacific Legal Foundation, including meetings and movement support.

He also states that he recruited three founders of the Federalist Society onto his staff.

So the current seated relations are:

```text
Heritage
-> support / convening / expansion
-> conservative public-interest legal ecology

Meese / Heritage personnel environment
-> personnel bridge
-> Federalist Society founders
```

The second is a personnel bridge, **not** proof that Heritage created or commands the Federalist Society.

## 5. Policy blueprints become a repeatable governing technology

Heritage's `Mandate for Leadership` model predates Project 2025 by decades.

Heritage says its 1980 Mandate was distributed to Reagan's Cabinet and that a substantial portion of its recommendations were adopted or attempted.

Whatever exact independent adoption percentage ultimately survives audit, the structural relation is clear:

```text
outside policy institution
-> administration-ready blueprint
-> personnel / transition relation
-> government implementation
```

This later reappears at much greater scale in Project 2025.

## 6. State replication is its own rail

### ALEC

ALEC describes a fifty-year model of supplying state legislators with policy solutions across labor, civil justice, education, economic, criminal-justice, and federalism issues.

The Lattice should trace:

```text
ALEC model text
-> named sponsor / staff / meeting / transmission event
-> state bill text
-> enactment
-> implementation
-> effect
```

Textual similarity alone is not enough for direct inheritance.

### State Policy Network

State Policy Network traces its origin to the 1986 Madison Group and formal establishment in 1992.

Its present self-description is particularly important because it explicitly names a **Durable Freedom Infrastructure** including:

```text
research
litigation
advocacy
investigative journalism
voter insights
leadership training
communications
policy working groups
fundraising support
```

It also describes a 50-state strategy designed to spread reforms from state to state and make policy wins survive election cycles.

This is not a hidden interpretation; it is the network's own stated operating model.

Current SPN board affiliations include DonorsTrust, the Coors Foundation, Stand Together, Heritage, and other institutions, creating real interlock edges that must still be typed separately as board, funding, personnel, or program relations.

## 10. A closed design-to-implementation chain: civil-service restructuring

This is currently one of the clearest modern transmission chains.

### Strategy seat

In a 2023 Heritage interview, Russ Vought explicitly identified Schedule F as a systemic reform that Project 2025 should make an institutional expectation for the next administration.

He described Project 2025 as movement-wide work intended to prepare people for granular executive-branch implementation.

### Personnel seat

Trump then selected Vought to return as OMB director.

### Authority / implementation seat

The administration created and implemented Schedule Policy/Career.

On June 3, 2026, Executive Order 14410 transferred designated policy-influencing career positions into the new excepted-service category.

The White House says approximately 8,000 positions were transferred and describes those positions as at-will, including removal for poor performance, misconduct, corruption, or `subversion of Presidential directives`.

So the evidence supports:

```text
Project 2025 strategy / Schedule F expectation
-> named policy carrier: Vought
-> OMB authority seat
-> second Trump administration
-> Schedule Policy/Career implementation
-> ~8,000 positions reclassified
```

## 11. The CPI / CRA / AFL / AAF cluster is a direct modern organizational network

Reuters' document- and tax-record-based investigation of the American Accountability Foundation provides an unusually concrete contemporary network trace.

Reported relations include material or organizational support involving:

```text
Conservative Partnership Institute
Center for Renewing America
America First Legal
Heritage Foundation
-> American Accountability Foundation
```

AAF later became a Project 2025 partner and created federal-worker watchlists designed to identify people it wanted removed from government positions.

Reuters documented fear, harassment, employment disruption, and departures among people listed, while not claiming the watchlist caused every individual government personnel action.

This creates two different edges:

```text
support / funding / organizational relation
!=
exact harm causation for every listed worker
```

Both belong in the Lattice.

## 12. The network is federated enough to fight with itself

A crucial discriminator is the current Trump / Leonard Leo / Federalist Society fracture.

Trump has publicly attacked Leo and criticized Federalist Society-linked judicial advice after judges constrained administration policies.

This means the model must allow:

```text
historical cooperation
+ shared legal infrastructure
+ prior judicial-selection relation
+ current factional conflict
```

rather than assuming either:

```text
one permanent command structure
```

or:

```text
current disagreement proves there was never a network
```

The fracture may itself mark a historical transition from older corporate/libertarian/originalist conservative infrastructure toward a more loyalty-centered MAGA executive-power formation.

That is a research question, not yet a completed conclusion.

## 14. What is already supported strongly enough to communicate publicly

A careful public statement can say:

> Over more than five decades, U.S. conservative and corporate political power has developed through overlapping legal, academic, philanthropic, think-tank, state-policy, personnel, and transition institutions. Some links are direct: the U.S. Chamber says its litigation center was created on Lewis Powell's recommendation; Heritage describes helping organize conservative public-interest law groups; Project 2025 formally coordinated 75 organizations around policy, personnel, training, and an implementation playbook; multiple Project-linked figures entered the second Trump administration; and at least one major Project 2025 priority—Schedule F-style civil-service restructuring—can be traced through a named architect into a realized 2026 federal mechanism. This does not mean every organization answers to one command center or that Powell personally caused every later development. It means the institutional lineage is sufficiently documented that treating present developments as isolated events is no longer an adequate model.

That statement should remain revisable as edges strengthen, weaken, or diverge.
