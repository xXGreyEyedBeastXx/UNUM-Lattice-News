## Purpose

This note begins calibrating the Harm Hierarchy Legibility Adversarial Audit against heterogeneous real cases.

It is intentionally **not** a final leaderboard. The goal is to test whether the new model can distinguish:

- realized death/injury;
- detention/coercion;
- material deprivation;
- rights/agency loss;
- Indigenous land/sovereignty loss;
- democratic/oversight destruction;
- concentrated state power;
- catastrophic war risk;
- direct operators from political/material enablers;
- confirmed conduct from projected or unresolved downstream harm.

Only a `CONFIRMED` scope may enter a future confirmed hierarchy. A broad allegation can remain `PENDING` while a narrower underlying act is `CONFIRMED`.

## Calibration Case 1 — 2026 U.S.-Iran war

### Scoped claim A

**Claim:** The 2026 U.S.-Iran war began with a joint U.S.-Israeli attack, under President Donald Trump and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, and by August 2026 had killed U.S. service members and thousands of Iranians while major U.S. objectives remained unmet.

**Investigation state:** `CONFIRMED` for the scoped chronology, U.S./Israeli initiation, reported casualties, and unresolved U.S. objectives.

**Claim posture:** observed / strong contemporaneous reporting.

**Shared harm id:** `shared-harm-2026-us-israel-iran-war`

**Candidate contributors:**
- United States executive / military institutions;
- Donald Trump — leader seat;
- Israeli government / military institutions;
- Benjamin Netanyahu — leader seat;
- later operational commanders and specific units to be separated.

**Attribution note:** leaders should normally begin at `R4 leadership / decisive enabling / command`; direct operational units may carry `R5` for their own actions. Do not duplicate the same Iranian or U.S. deaths independently into every contributor.

**Current harm vector direction:**
- lethal/physical: extreme;
- material deprivation / infrastructure/economic disruption: serious, requires fuller quantification;
- power concentration: severe military/executive pathway;
- catastrophic risk: high-priority active escalation lane, requires separate pathway-support scoring.

**Sources:**
- Reuters, `Trump's Iran dilemma: He's stuck in war with no exit in sight`, 2026-08-06: https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trumps-iran-dilemma-hes-stuck-war-with-no-exit-sight-2026-08-06/

- Reuters, current ceasefire/war reporting, 2026-08-11 to 2026-08-12.

### Path forward

- build casualty chronology from U.S., Iranian, hospital, humanitarian, and independent conflict-monitoring records;
- separate combatant/civilian deaths where evidence permits;
- map strikes by actor and command authority;

## Calibration Case 3 — ICE detention deaths under second Trump administration

### Scoped claim A

**Claim:** Reuters found 50 deaths in ICE detention from Trump's January 2025 return through early June 2026, with a detention death rate about one death per 1,630 detainees versus one per 3,848 annually from 2009-2024.

**Investigation state:** `CONFIRMED` for the Reuters analysis of ICE records.

**Claim posture:** observed / investigative quantitative analysis based on ICE data.

**Institutional actor:** ICE / DHS detention system.

**Important causal boundary:** `death in custody` is not automatically identical to `death caused by ICE`. Individual causal contribution, medical neglect, suicide prevention failure, or other mechanism must be resolved case by case.

**Source:**
- Reuters, `Death rate in ICE immigrant detention centers more than doubles under Trump`, 2026-06-17: https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/death-rate-ice-immigrant-detention-centers-more-than-doubles-under-trump-reuters-2026-06-17/

### Scoped claim B

**Claim:** ICE detention is a civil detention system that includes private facilities and facility oversight standards.

**Investigation state:** `CONFIRMED`.

**Source:**
- ICE detention overview: https://www.ice.gov/detain

### Path forward

- one contribution page per death;
- medical chronology, known conditions, requests for care, response time, facility, contractor, cause/manner of death, inspection history;
- compare mortality by facility/operator and population denominator;
- trace Trump/DHS policy contribution through detention expansion separately from facility-level causal responsibility;
- audit the 2026 policy change ending reporting of deaths within 30 days after release.

# What this calibration already teaches

## 1. Realized harm and structural harm cannot be one column

ICE custody deaths and an active war contain realized human endpoints.

Bears Ears governance removal, oversight contraction, police-power expansion, and arms-transfer safeguard rescission contain confirmed structural changes whose full downstream victim counts are not yet known.

The hierarchy must display both without pretending the second category has already produced every feared endpoint.

## 2. Forecast deprivation must not be scored as completed deprivation

CBO estimates are evidence about expected consequences, not evidence that all projected people have already lost coverage or food assistance.

The enacted mechanism can be `CONFIRMED`; future realized harm remains `PATH_FORWARD` until measured.

## 3. Leader and institution attribution must stay separate

A president may sign, order, direct, appoint, fund, or politically normalize an institutional mechanism without personally operating every downstream action.

Likewise, an institution can have direct operational responsibility that exceeds the leader's contribution to a particular incident.

## 4. Shared harm deduplication is necessary

The 2026 Iran war is the clearest calibration case. U.S. and Israeli leaders, governments, militaries, units, and contractors may all have contribution records, but the same death cannot become five different deaths in the hierarchy.

## 5. Partisan symmetry is operational, not rhetorical

The Biden arms-transfer record remains in the harm audit because the government continued weapons support after its own human-rights concerns became substantial enough to trigger formal review.

The Trump record separately includes removal of safeguards and continued support.

Harris's record currently supports a narrower political-position contribution than Biden's executive arms-transfer authority. The audit should become harsher if stronger evidence emerges, not by silently inflating her authority now.
