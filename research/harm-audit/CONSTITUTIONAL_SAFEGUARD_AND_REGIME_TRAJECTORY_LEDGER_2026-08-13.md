## Purpose

This ledger is not a name-calling device and is not intended to prove that the current United States is already equivalent to Nazi Germany, Stalin's Soviet Union, Putin's Russia, or Netanyahu's Israel.

It asks a narrower and more falsifiable set of questions:

1. Which constitutional, statutory, institutional, and civic safeguards are supposed to constrain public power?
2. Which of those safeguards are currently functioning, degraded, bypassed, pressured, captured, or removed?
3. When the administration encounters resistance, does it accept the constraint, appeal within the existing system, seek lawful structural change, weaken the constraint, replace personnel, reroute authority, attack the constraint's legitimacy, or return with another mechanism aimed at substantially the same result?
4. How often does a stated preference recur across speeches, orders, litigation, appointments, funding decisions, enforcement priorities, and institutional design?
5. Which historical or contemporary regimes used comparable mechanisms, and where do those comparisons materially break?
6. What human consequences follow from each institutional change?
7. What would count as genuine reversal, restoration, or successful re-entry?

The comparison is therefore mechanism-first:

```text
preferred direction
-> repetition / persistence
-> formal proposal or order
-> personnel or resource commitment
-> operational preparation
-> implementation
-> resistance encountered
-> response to resistance
-> realized consequence
-> correction / reversal / recurrence
```

A blocked attempt remains evidence of trajectory. It is not counted as realized harm.

# I. Core locks

```text
historical analogy != identity
similar mechanism != equivalent regime
stated preference != implemented policy
implemented policy != every downstream harm
blocked attempt != no evidence about direction
repetition != independent corroboration
extremist praise != executive control of extremist organization
court loss != court capture
court victory for executive != court capture
institutional pressure != institutional destruction
competitive election != automatically free and fair election
nominal institution != effective constraint
formal legality != constitutional legitimacy
constitutional disagreement != proof of bad faith
protective rationale != automatic benign effect
harmful effect != automatic proof of private intent
```

The comparison must preserve both convergence and divergence.

# II. Evidence ladder for preferred direction

Use a separate posture for each mechanism.

## D0 — no supported directional evidence

No recoverable evidence that the actor seeks the scoped institutional change.

## D1 — isolated rhetoric or preference

A statement favors or gestures toward a more coercive, less pluralistic, less independent, or more leader-controlled arrangement, but no repeated pattern or formal mechanism is yet present.

## D2 — repeated stated preference

The same directional preference appears across multiple dates or contexts.

Do not count ten repetitions of one speech line as ten independent pieces of evidence. Persistence matters when the preference survives time, criticism, institutional resistance, or changing contexts.

## D3 — formal institutional attempt

The preference becomes an executive order, regulation, bill request, appointment strategy, funding action, prosecution demand, litigation position, emergency declaration, reclassification, deployment, or other formal state action.

## D4 — operational preparation / capacity construction

Personnel, funding, databases, detention capacity, enforcement guidance, institutional restructuring, legal interpretations, partnerships, or other machinery materially increase the actor's ability to execute the preferred direction.

## D5 — implemented structural change

The mechanism materially changes how public power is exercised.

## D6 — durable constraint displacement

The prior independent safeguard has lost practical capacity to block, review, expose, or reverse the exercise of power.

D6 is a high bar. It should not be assigned merely because the administration won a case, appointed ideologically aligned officials, or temporarily prevailed.

# IV. Constitutional safeguard map — United States, August 2026

The status labels below are provisional research states, not final constitutional holdings.

## 1. Presidency / executive self-restraint

### Assigned function

The President executes federal law within constitutional and statutory bounds while sharing power with Congress and remaining subject to judicial review.

### Current pressure indicators

#### Centralized control over independent agencies

The February 2025 executive framework titled **Ensuring Accountability for All Agencies** extended White House regulatory review and centralized executive supervision over agencies historically designed with degrees of independence. OMB implementation guidance followed in April 2025.

Posture:

```yaml
centralization_direction: CONFIRMED
formal_action: CONFIRMED
full_elimination_of_independent_agency_constraints: NOT_ESTABLISHED
```

#### Schedule Policy/Career

The January 20, 2025 executive order reinstated the Schedule F concept under the renamed Schedule Policy/Career system. It expressly states that policy-influencing career employees must faithfully implement administration policy and may be dismissed for failure to do so. OPM finalized implementing rules in 2026, and a June 3, 2026 executive order placed about 8,000 senior policy-influencing positions into the category.

Counterevidence / narrowing:

- OPM states that the positions remain career and merit-based.
- The framework formally preserves prohibitions on whistleblower retaliation, discrimination, and other prohibited personnel practices, though enforcement arrangements changed.
- Political affiliation is not formally made a hiring requirement.

Posture:

```yaml
preference_for_greater_presidential_personnel_control: CONFIRMED
operational_implementation: CONFIRMED
conversion_of_all_civil_service_to_political_patronage: NOT_ESTABLISHED
```

### Current status

**SYSTEMIC PRESSURE / PARTIAL STRUCTURAL IMPLEMENTATION**

## 2. Judiciary

### Assigned function

Independent adjudication, constitutional review, due process enforcement, and lawful resolution of disputes involving executive and legislative power.

### Evidence that the constraint still functions

#### Birthright citizenship

In *Trump v. Barbara* on June 30, 2026, the Supreme Court held that the affected children are citizens at birth under the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship Clause, defeating the administration's contrary executive position on the merits.

#### Alien Enemies Act removals

In the A.A.R.P. litigation, the Supreme Court enjoined removals of the putative detainee class while requiring lower-court consideration of what notice due process requires and whether the Alien Enemies Act authorized the removals at issue.

These are direct examples of judicial constraint operating against the executive.

### Pressure indicators

The broader 2025-26 litigation record includes repeated executive efforts to obtain emergency relief, disputes over judicial authority, and public attacks on adverse judicial rulings. These must be extracted case-by-case before stronger claims about noncompliance or institutional capture are assigned.

### Current status

**FUNCTIONING UNDER HEAVY PRESSURE**

Do not classify the federal judiciary or Supreme Court as captured while they continue to issue and enforce consequential rulings against the administration.

Track separately whether doctrine concerning removal authority, immunity, emergency relief, standing, universal injunctions, administrative control, or other areas increases executive power.

## 3. Congress

### Assigned function

Legislation, appropriations, oversight, impeachment, confirmation, and constitutional war powers.

### War-powers example

Congressional resolutions concerning Iran explicitly state that Congress has not declared war on Iran or enacted a specific statutory authorization for the relevant hostilities. Members of both chambers have introduced measures directing removal of U.S. armed forces from unauthorized hostilities.

This supports a narrower finding than "Congress has disappeared":

```text
constitutional concern identified
-> legislative resistance attempted
-> institution has not consistently made the constraint effective
```

The distinction matters because failed checking is not the same as absence of checking.

### Other investigation lanes

- withholding or redirection of congressionally appropriated funds;
- emergency authorities used around ordinary legislative processes;
- congressional acquiescence, active support, or resistance;
- oversight hearings, subpoenas, inspector-general protection, and enforcement;
- Senate confirmation behavior where independence of law-enforcement or regulatory institutions is at issue.

### Current status

**FUNCTIONING, BUT INCONSISTENT AND OFTEN INEFFECTIVE AS A CHECK**

This status requires issue-specific decomposition rather than a single partisan score.

## 4. Department of Justice and federal law enforcement

### Assigned function

Enforce federal law consistently with the Constitution and statutes, preserve due process, protect civil rights, investigate federal crime, defend the United States in litigation, and maintain internal mechanisms capable of detecting misconduct and unlawful conduct.

### Priority trajectory questions

Track:

- presidential or White House demands concerning named investigative/prosecutorial targets;
- appointment or replacement of prosecutors after resistance;
- case initiation after prior prosecutors decline;
- judicial findings involving appointment legality or prosecutorial authority;
- staffing and authority of DOJ OPR, DOJ OIG, FBI inspection/oversight, civil-rights enforcement, and professional-responsibility systems;
- whether different ideological categories receive systematically different investigative thresholds;
- retaliation against internal dissenters or whistleblowers;
- whether public claims of "weaponization" are followed by neutral institutional reform or by selective reorientation of enforcement power.

### Current status

**MATERIAL DEGRADATION CONCERN — REQUIRES CASE-BY-CASE ADJUDICATION**

Do not convert concern about politicization into an unsupported claim that every DOJ investigation is politically directed.

## 5. Domestic-terrorism / political-violence protection layer

### Assigned function

Detect, prevent, investigate, and prosecute politically motivated violence without converting political identity or lawful dissent into criminal guilt.

### Directional evidence

In September 2025, the White House formally designated "Antifa" a domestic terrorist organization and directed federal agencies to investigate, disrupt, and dismantle illegal operations attributed to Antifa or persons claiming to act on its behalf. A subsequent presidential memorandum described a wider domestic-terrorism framework that associated the targeted threat environment with anti-Americanism, anti-capitalism, anti-Christianity, migration, race, gender, and opposition to traditional views.

This is significant because it moves a broad ideological field toward a national-security / organized-crime enforcement framework.

Required counterevidence:

- actual violent conduct must remain distinguishable from lawful anti-fascist, left-wing, anti-capitalist, secular, pro-migrant, racial-justice, or gender-rights advocacy;
- violent actors should not receive immunity because their ideology is left-wing;
- the existence of left-wing political violence does not establish that a diffuse political label constitutes a coherent national organization;
- white-supremacist, neo-Nazi, anti-government, religious, incel, jihadist, and other violent-extremist threats must be compared using the same conduct-based standard.

### Current status

**IDEOLOGICAL-OVERBREADTH RISK / ENFORCEMENT-ASYMMETRY INVESTIGATION ACTIVE**

# V. Comparative regime mechanics

The comparison is not a moral ranking. It asks which safeguards failed, how they failed, and what endpoint followed.

## A. Hitler / Nazi Germany — rapid coordination and elimination of pluralism

USHMM's account of *Gleichschaltung* documents a rapid sequence in 1933:

```text
emergency decree suspends civil liberties / due process
-> Enabling Act permits lawmaking without parliament, including laws conflicting with constitution
-> state governments subordinated
-> civil service purged of Jews and political opponents
-> judges, teachers, professors, lawyers and other institutions aligned
-> competing political parties prohibited
-> one-party state
```

This is an endpoint the current United States has **not** reached.

The useful comparison is whether early mechanisms of executive concentration, civil-service loyalty pressure, emergency framing, opposition delegitimation, or attacks on independent institutions begin to reduce the number of effective veto points.

### Comparison break

Current U.S. conditions still include competitive elections, opposition governments, independent media, adversarial civil society, federalism, and courts issuing consequential rulings against the executive.

Do not collapse these differences.

---

## B. Stalin / Soviet Union — total party-state and coercive apparatus fusion

Library of Congress archival material describes Stalin as having eliminated likely political opposition by the mid-1930s and then using purges, widespread arrests, executions, labor camps, censorship, forced deportation, and direct secret-police power to create comprehensive submission to party-state rule.

### Useful comparison

Stalin functions primarily as an **outer-bound failure model**:

- independent political competition gone;
- independent judiciary gone;
- independent press gone;
- secret police directly serving leadership;
- mass detention and forced labor;
- mass political killing;
- no realistic peaceful re-entry path.

### Comparison break

The present U.S. system is nowhere near this level of coercive integration or political terror.

Therefore Stalin should not be used as a rhetorical synonym for ordinary executive overreach. He is useful as a measure of what complete destruction of independent return paths looks like.

---

## C. Putin / Russian Federation — gradual hollowing while institutional shells remain

Freedom House's 2026 Russia assessment describes power concentrated in the presidency, subservient courts and security forces, controlled national media, a legislature dominated by the ruling party and pliable opposition, manipulated elections, and extensive suppression of genuine opposition through extremism, foreign-agent, undesirable-organization, and related legal mechanisms.

This creates the most useful modern trajectory comparison because many formal institutions remain visible while their independent capacity is severely reduced.

### Key discriminator

```text
Can a genuine opposition movement organize, speak, obtain media access, contest elections, survive prosecution pressure, win, take office, and peacefully remove the governing network?
```

Where the formal answer remains "yes" but practical conditions make it increasingly unrealistic, institutional shell and functional constraint have diverged.

### Comparison break

The United States still has meaningful opposition control at state/local levels, national electoral competition, independent media, strong civil society, and an independent judiciary with real enforcement capacity.

---

## D. Netanyahu / Israel — democratic contestation under severe institutional and wartime pressure

Freedom House's 2026 Israel assessment continues to describe competitive political institutions while warning that judicial independence has come under increasing political pressure. The governing coalition's judicial-appointments changes and continuing institutional conflict provide a useful comparison for an elected government seeking greater control while courts, civil society, opposition parties, and elections remain meaningful constraints.

This comparison must keep two analytical lanes separate:

1. **internal democratic / constitutional structure within Israel proper**;
2. **military occupation, Gaza war, Palestinian rights, civilian harm, displacement, detention, and international-law accountability**.

Conflating the two produces bad analysis.

### Comparison break

Israel's war/occupation environment and the legal status of Palestinians create state-power and humanitarian conditions that do not map cleanly onto U.S. domestic governance.

# VII. Persistence and recurrence index

For every preferred-direction claim, count **episodes**, not quotations.

An episode is a distinct institutional event such as:

- new speech after prior controversy;
- executive order;
- agency rule;
- litigation position;
- appointment or firing;
- funding action;
- deployment;
- prosecution demand;
- emergency declaration;
- legislative proposal;

### Why recurrence matters

A single overbroad proposal may be error, improvisation, or failed legal theory.

Repeated recurrence across unrelated institutional lanes can support a stronger systems inference:

```text
independent constraint encountered
-> constraint described as illegitimate / obstructive
-> authority centralized
-> resistant personnel made more removable
-> alternate legal basis sought
-> funding / jurisdiction shifted
-> objective reappears
```

This pattern must be demonstrated, not assumed.

# VIII. Humanitarian consequence layer

Constitutional degradation matters because institutional safeguards protect bodies, livelihoods, identities, liberty, political agency, and the ability to seek repair.

For every safeguard failure, ask:

```text
Who loses refusal power?
Who loses appeal?
Who becomes easier to detain, deport, prosecute, surveil, defund, fire, silence, dispossess, or exclude?
Who receives new discretionary power?
What evidence exists of actual injury?
What downstream harm is foreseeable but not yet realized?
What institution can still reverse the harm?
```

This links the constitutional ledger to the existing Trump Humanitarian Harm and Extremist Enablement Ledger without turning constitutional doctrine into an abstract elite dispute.

# IX. Worsening indicators

Treat the following as high-priority trigger events if supported by recoverable evidence:

1. open refusal to comply with final court judgments;
2. removal, arrest, prosecution, or disabling of judges because of adverse rulings;
3. statutory or practical elimination of opposition-party electoral competition;
4. systematic exclusion of opposition voters or candidates outside ordinary adjudicated eligibility rules;
5. federal criminalization of broad political identity rather than specific unlawful conduct;
6. political loyalty as an explicit condition for ordinary career public employment;
7. security forces used against lawful opposition activity as such;
8. closure, licensing suppression, or coerced control of major independent media because of political criticism;
9. mass preventive detention of political opponents;
10. cancellation, indefinite postponement, or nullification of competitive national elections;
11. executive refusal to relinquish office after a final lawful electoral result;
12. court, Congress, inspector-general, civil-service, press, state, and civil-society constraints failing simultaneously in the same policy lane;
13. political violence receiving material state protection or impunity because it serves the governing faction;
14. emergency powers repeatedly renewed after the factual emergency has ended;
15. legal equality replaced by explicit hereditary, racial, religious, ideological, or party-status hierarchy in citizenship or basic civil standing.

No single indicator automatically establishes a historical regime identity. Several would represent major loss of democratic re-entry capacity.

# X. Improvement / re-entry indicators

The model must be capable of recording recovery.

Positive re-entry evidence includes:

- compliance with final adverse judgments;
- abandonment of blocked coercive mechanisms;
- restoration of inspector-general / professional-responsibility staffing and independence;
- renewed congressional authorization discipline for war and emergency power;
- protection of politically neutral civil-service processes;
- restoration of viewpoint-neutral domestic-terrorism and political-violence enforcement;
- transparent correction of false executive claims;
- protection of press and protest rights across ideological lines;
- independent investigations permitted to reach outcomes adverse to the governing coalition;
- peaceful acceptance of election losses;
- removal of emergency measures when triggering conditions end;
- repair or compensation for people unlawfully harmed by state action.

A trajectory model that cannot register improvement is propaganda, not analysis.

# XI. Current working conclusion

As of August 13, 2026, the evidence does **not** support classifying the United States as institutionally equivalent to Nazi Germany after Gleichschaltung, Stalin's Soviet Union, or Putin's present-day Russia.

The evidence **does** support sustained investigation of a repeated preferred direction toward greater presidential control, reduced independence of policy-influencing personnel and agencies, broader use of emergency/security frameworks, politicization risk in law enforcement, pressure on independent institutions, and repeated attempts to expand executive discretion.

The most important live question is not whether a historical label can be attached today.

It is:

> **How many independent institutions still possess practical power to say no, make the no stick, protect the person under consequence, and provide a return path after abuse?**

And, when those institutions say no:

> **Does the administration accept the boundary, or does it repeatedly seek to weaken, bypass, replace, or delegitimize the boundary until the same objective becomes executable?**

That is the trajectory to measure.
