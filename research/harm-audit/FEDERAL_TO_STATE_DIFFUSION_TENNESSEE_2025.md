## Bounded question

What can be established about the relationship between the second Trump administration's immigration agenda and Tennessee's 2025 state immigration-enforcement restructuring, and which harms or power changes belong to Trump, Tennessee leaders, the state legislature, state agencies, local governments, or federal agencies?

## Confirmed federal-to-state agenda edge

On January 22, 2025, Tennessee Governor Bill Lee's office announced a special-session immigration agenda and explicitly connected it to the incoming Trump administration.

The governor's office stated that Trump's day-one executive actions were the federal context for the Tennessee proposal and quoted Lee saying:

> President Trump has made it clear that states will play a major role in partnering with his Administration ... and Tennessee is heeding the call.

The same release said the proposed state Chief Immigration Enforcement Officer would coordinate directly with the Trump administration on federal immigration policy and implementation.

**Investigation state:** `CONFIRMED` for political/agenda diffusion and intended coordination.

**Do not overstate:** this source does not prove Trump personally drafted Tennessee bill language or ordered every provision.

Primary source:
- Tennessee Governor, 2025-01-22: https://www.tn.gov/governor/news/2025/1/22/gov--lee--legislative-leadership-present-agenda-on-illegal-immigration-ahead-of-special-session.html

## Confirmed Tennessee law

HB6001 / SB6002 became Public Chapter 1 in February 2025.

The Tennessee General Assembly's official summary states that the enacted law:

- created a centralized immigration enforcement division within the Department of Safety;
- created a grant program promoting enforcement of federal immigration laws;
- created criminal penalties for officials who adopt sanctuary policies and required removal from office upon conviction;
- changed state identification rules for lawful permanent residents and related voter-eligibility administration;
- created structures for state/local participation in federal immigration programs.

**Investigation state:** `CONFIRMED`.

Primary sources:
- HB6001: https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=HB6001&ga=

## Harm / power lanes

### Confirmed structural changes

- new centralized state immigration-enforcement institution;
- state grants designed to support federal immigration-law enforcement;
- increased state/local participation pathway for federal immigration programs;
- criminal liability/removal consequences for specified local-official sanctuary-policy conduct;
- additional state administrative classification of noncitizen residents.

These are eligible for structural-harm review because they increase state/federal enforcement capacity and reduce local resistance/exit within the policy system.

### Downstream harms requiring proof

`PATH_FORWARD`:

- arrests or detention caused by new state/local participation;
- wrongful detention or citizenship-status errors;
- racial/ethnic profiling;
- victims/witnesses avoiding police or services;
- local officials chilled or prosecuted under sanctuary provisions;
- grant-funded equipment or personnel used in specific enforcement actions;
- family separation, employment, housing, healthcare, or education consequences;
- effect of temporary-license markers or citizenship documentation rules;
- interaction with Native/tribal citizens and IDs;
- due-process and equal-protection litigation.

Do not count these as realized harm until case/data evidence exists.
